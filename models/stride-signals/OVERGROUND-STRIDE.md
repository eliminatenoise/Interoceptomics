# One slow overground stride

Start when the right foot lands. Stop the next time it lands. In this sample of barefoot adults, that stride took **1.70 seconds on average**. Reflective markers traced the movement, electrodes picked up electrical activity from eight muscles on the right, and two plates measured the force that reached the floor. These instruments recorded the same walks. Each curve averages many walks, so the moving figure represents a group. [Study and measurement methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0124-4)

| What the display shows | Who contributes |
| --- | --- |
| 52 skin markers in metres | 29 or 30 people per marker and phase |
| Eight right-leg electrical amplitudes in microvolts | 30 people at each phase |
| Vertical load on physical plate 1 or 2 in newtons | 29 people on plate 1, 28 on plate 2 |
| Three directions of force under the verified right foot | 3 people at 2–58% of the stride |

**Reading the footfall.** At 0% and 100%, the same foot reaches the ground. Right foot-off occurred at 68.1% on average (between-person SD 2.3 percentage points). People took different amounts of time, so a one-percent step is a fraction of each person's stride. Spread is the sample standard deviation across people's average values. It describes differences between walkers after alignment.

**Reading the floor.** Each hardware curve follows one physical plate. Walkers traversed the walkway in either direction and could place either foot on a given plate. A zero on an available plate means that plate recorded no load above the source processing floor. The foot could be carrying weight elsewhere. The two plate curves also use different sets of trials and people. Recovering a walker’s total force would require simultaneous coverage of every loaded foot for that walker. The separate right-contact curves required a documented plate assignment, agreement with the recorded events and a foot-position check. Only four strides passed all three checks. Values outside 2–58% are withheld for insufficient support. Three people give a narrow reference for this contact, especially for horizontal shear.

**Reading the electrodes.** Each trace measures the amplitude of voltage recorded at the skin. The electrodes sampled one leg, so they offer no measurement of the left muscles. Electrode placement, tissue and individual anatomy affect voltage. Muscle force, joint torque, nerve traffic and sensation would each require separate measurements. The source had already filtered the signals. A centered amplitude window uses samples on both sides of the plot cursor.

## Where does the marker go in 200 ms?

At the halfway point of the stride, the marker on the outside of the right ankle moved forward **56.9 mm**, right **9.7 mm** and up **65.4 mm** over the next fifth of a second. Those are three components of one mean movement. The straight-line distance between the two ankle positions averaged **88.2 mm** across the same walks. An ankle can turn on its way between them. Measuring its full path would require adding the distances along that curve. Here we connect only the two ends.

The input is the version 8 optical record, which the original authors gap-filled and smoothed. We took two positions from the stored 100 Hz frames for each of 52 markers. A pair starts at the nearest frame inside the stride to the selected percentage, breaking ties toward the later frame. We treat a timestamp within 0.0001 of an integer or half-integer frame as exact to allow for binary event-time rounding. The second measurement lies exactly 20 frames later. It must fall at or before the second right strike. We never wrap around to the next stride. Strides that are longer than others have more time left near the same percentage. The last few arrows therefore represent a progressively smaller group.

Both endpoint samples must pass the missing-marker rules below. An absent endpoint removes the whole pair at that phase. A gap between two valid ends does not change their separation. The normalized display supplies the phase cursor. All 200 ms endpoint values come from the stored frames. The same two right-strike events, pelvis origin, floor height and per-trial forward, left and up directions used for the figure also align each pair. These are fixed walkway axes, so forward movement of the whole body counts.

For each trial we retain the aligned start position, the signed displacement on each axis and the Euclidean distance between the two ends. Within each walker we average each quantity over their available trials. We then give each contributing walker equal weight. Taking the distance before averaging counts movements that would otherwise cancel in the mean arrow. Its green ring starts at the mean of the paired starts, which can differ from the yellow position on the full-stride trace. The tip is that paired mean end. The arrow rotates with the body. A movement that points toward the camera appears shorter on the screen.

We planned 90 strides across 30 walkers. One stride failed the anatomical axis check, leaving 89. We retain the actual trial and walker counts at every phase for every marker. The group readouts require at least three walkers. At the right ankle, phase 89% has four, phase 90% has two, and by 93% there are none. Values are descriptive of these slow, barefoot walks. The measurements support neither a forecast nor a force or nerve reading.

## Where the positions come from

Schreiber and Moissenet placed 52 reflective markers on landmarks of the feet, legs, pelvis, trunk, shoulders, arms and hands. Their cameras sampled at 100 Hz. They reconstructed missing marker paths and smoothed them at 6 Hz before releasing the files. The distributed trajectories do not identify every upstream reconstruction. We retained the recorded landmarks and joined selected pairs with lines that help the eye follow each limb. A marker lies on the skin. Locating a bone axis or joint centre would require an additional anatomical model. C7 is a neck marker. The head has no measured position in this set. The two sternal markers and the scapular SIA markers retain neutral site labels because the source names and their relative heights do not support a more specific anatomical assignment consistently. [Marker table, figure and processing](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0124-4)

A metre in the display is a metre in the source walk. We converted millimetres to metres and put the horizontal origin at the mean of the four pelvic markers at the first right strike. The horizontal displacement of that same pelvic centre to the next strike defines forward for the trial. Up follows laboratory Z. Up crossed with forward defines the walker’s left. The height origin uses the mean top-plane corner height when both plates have valid horizontal planes, otherwise laboratory zero. The figure retains the walk across the floor. The pelvic centre advances approximately 1.02 m across the group-average stride.

## Reproducing the reference

Use release 8 of the dataset and Supplementary Table 2 from the article. The dataset contains 50 uninjured adults and five walking conditions. This reference uses the group set aside for training in a companion study. We sorted the source participant folders, selected the first 30 people and took their first three available C2 repeats in numerical order. C2 is a metronome-guided, barefoot, straight overground walk with a target speed of 0.4–0.8 m/s. The authors verified the target on the first trial. We did not select recordings by their measured speeds. The other 20 people contribute no signal. [Version 8 data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.7734767.v8) · [Article and supplementary materials](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6610108/#MOESM2)

1. Use the C3D event context “Right” and labels “Foot Strike1” and “Foot Strike2” for the two ends. Require a stride between 0.5 and 4 s and one intervening right foot-off and left strike. The original operator marked events using vertical force where available and marker motion elsewhere. Keep the fractional event times and sample the stride at 101 equally spaced phases. This alignment is retrospective and serves the display. The paired forecast uses its own time windows. [Source event export](https://github.com/fmoissenet/CGA_Rehazenter_Toolbox/blob/e35dc90c1acd24da134897fd1b4241152cff6005/exportEvents.m)
2. Require the four pelvic anchors at both ends and at least 0.20 m of horizontal progress. The median left anterior pelvic marker must lie at least 0.05 m left of the right one, and median C7 height must exceed each heel by more than 0.65 m. A negative residual, an all-zero coordinate or a nonfinite coordinate masks that marker and frame. Use adjacent valid frames for linear interpolation. One of 90 trials lacked the required anatomical evidence, leaving 89 eligible strides from 30 people. Stride duration had a between-person SD of 0.17 s.
3. The acquisition software synchronized cameras, electrodes and plates. The published files contain 15 analog samples for each optical frame. On the authors' centered crop convention, zero-based optical frame *i* corresponds to analog sample *15i + 7*. At each phase take the nearest such analog time. For each available, nonconstant EMG channel, square 75 consecutive finite samples centered on that time, average them, take the square root, and convert volts to microvolts. The window spans 50 ms of the supplied 30–300 Hz band-passed signal. No calibration to a maximal contraction was available here. The files support a common nominal clock. The sensor-specific delays remain unverified. [Marker crop](https://github.com/fmoissenet/CGA_Rehazenter_Toolbox/blob/e35dc90c1acd24da134897fd1b4241152cff6005/importTrialMarker.m) · [EMG processing](https://github.com/fmoissenet/CGA_Rehazenter_Toolbox/blob/e35dc90c1acd24da134897fd1b4241152cff6005/importTrialEMG.m)
4. Read the two Type 2 force platforms with ezc3d, in laboratory coordinates, and average 15 samples centered on each phase. Ground reaction is positive upward. Check that reconstructed vertical force agrees with the negative of the stored local Fz channel within 0.1 N. Rotate the two horizontal force components into the same walking axes used for the markers. In Supplementary Table 2, the first digit identifies the right foot's plate and the second identifies the left foot's plate. Zero denotes unavailable or unreliable information. Retain a plate only when the table assigns it to at least one foot, the signal is finite, and the plate records a load reaching 50 N during the trial. A missing exact supplement entry gives no plate assignment. These checks retain 75 trials for plate 1 and 70 for plate 2. The article reports a 5 N force threshold. The authors' released force-processing code uses 50 N after a 15 Hz low-pass filter. We preserve the archived values and their zeros. [Force processing and threshold](https://github.com/fmoissenet/CGA_Rehazenter_Toolbox/blob/e35dc90c1acd24da134897fd1b4241152cff6005/importTrialReaction.m)
5. For a specifically right-side contact, require the source right-side plate assignment to agree with one complete, positive vertical-load bout lasting at least 0.20 s. Its observed onset must be within 0.12 s of the first right strike and its offset within 0.15 s of right foot-off. At the middle of the bout, the right heel and first, second and fifth metatarsal markers must all lie over the plate in the horizontal plane with 15 mm edge tolerance. The centroid of those four left-foot markers must lie outside that polygon and tolerance. This conservative plan-view check can reject a left foot passing above the plate. For a unique qualifying bout, retain only samples whose full averaging window lies within the bout.
6. At each phase, average available trials for each person, then average those people with equal weight. For each series, the JSON gives the mean, the sample standard deviation across people, and the number of distinct people contributing at that phase. A marker has three means and three component-wise standard deviations. Values with fewer than three people are missing and carry their observed count. The source group and one speed condition limit comparisons to other walks or clinical populations.

The figure replays an average assembled from measurements. A forecast shown alongside it must be judged against observations that were set aside before fitting.

### Run the calculation

The script accepts the original data ZIP and the publisher’s supplementary Word file (Supplementary materials, filename ending in `MOESM2_ESM.docx`). It checks both file fingerprints, reads only the selected walking records and writes the aggregate JSON. The verified environment used Python 3.12, NumPy 2.5.5 and ezc3d 1.7.2.

```sh
python -m pip install 'numpy==2.5.5' 'ezc3d==1.7.2'
python build_stride.py "A multimodal dataset of human gait at different walking speeds.zip" \
  41597_2019_124_MOESM2_ESM.docx stride-reference.json
```

At 50% of the stride, the group mean tibialis anterior amplitude is 12.7499 µV (SD 17.2587 µV, 30 people). The two plate means at that phase are 381.9093 N (SD 252.7079 N, 29 people) and 321.2284 N (SD 257.0334 N, 28 people). These values were checked against a second calculation directly from the selected source files.

### Rebuild the fixed-time measurements

From the repository or extracted package root, run the following with the same Python environment used for the stride reference. The walking ZIP is the publisher's original version 8 archive. The program checks its SHA-256 and decodes only the three selected C2 walks for each of the first 30 participants. An existing directory containing exactly these 90 source files can be passed with `--train-dir` instead. The program verifies the combined hash of the selected records in both cases.

```sh
python models/stride-signals/build_travel.py --archive /path/to/walking.zip
python models/stride-signals/verify_travel.py
python docs/stride-signals/build.py
```

The published table retains no individual rows. It stores the paired start, signed movement and mean endpoint distance in millimetres, rounded to 0.001 mm for numerical reproduction. That precision describes the export, not the optical instrument. A second implementation decoded the original C3D records separately and matched every component and distance within 0.000500 mm, every support count and every withheld value. The local checker verifies the published file, its numerical constraints and a worked ankle result.
