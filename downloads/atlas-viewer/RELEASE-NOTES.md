# Four passages of the atlas viewer's guide made clearer

The Interoceptomics Atlas Viewer, version of 27 November 2026.

[Open it on the website](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/Interoceptomics/atlas/) · [Download it](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/Interoceptomics/raw/main/downloads/atlas-viewer/Interoceptomics-Atlas-Viewer.zip) (ZIP, 64.3 MB) · [Checksum](Interoceptomics-Atlas-Viewer.zip.sha256) · [How to open the download](../../README.md#use-it-offline-download-it) · [Licences and credits](../../RIGHTS.md)

Four passages of the guide, and one of the page about where the anatomy comes from, are made clearer: [the guide, Simple edition](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/Interoceptomics/atlas/simple.html) · [the guide, Technical edition](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/Interoceptomics/atlas/technical.html). The viewer, its 3D model, its regions, its colours and its controls are as they were in the version before, of 25 November 2026.

- **In the Simple edition's words.** When you pick a region from the list, the view can change. The guide now names the regions that leave the view as it is: VPL, VPM, the red nucleus and the twenty regions drawn only when you ask. It says that the parietal operculum changes the view in the same way as a region of the cortex under **Part of a larger surface**, and no longer gives it two different results. In the table for when the viewer does not start, "can't open file" with the name `launch.py` leads to the row about the start file only after you used a start file. After `python3 -B launch.py` typed in another folder, it leads to the row about moving the terminal into the viewer's folder. The Simple guide says not to put double quotes around a path that you dragged into a terminal: the terminal writes it in the form that `cd` needs. The blue and the copper of VPL and VPM, in their comparison and in their cuts, only tell the two regions apart. They are not the colour groups, and the pages now say so.
- **In the Technical edition's words.** Both guides name the regions that keep the view when picked (VPL, VPM, the red nucleus and the twenty drawn only when asked) and state that the parietal operculum, which is also part of the brain's outer surface, acts as a cortical region under **Part of a larger surface** does. The trouble row for "can't open file" with `launch.py` applies after using a start file, so `python3 -B launch.py` typed in another folder leads to the row on `cd`. The Simple guide says that a path dragged into a terminal needs no double quotes added. The guides and the page on where the anatomy comes from state that the blue and copper of VPL and VPM are not the colour groups.

Run for this version, on Linux in headless Chromium 155, from a copy of the website served on this computer and from an unpacked copy of the download served the same way: the viewer's first view, compared pixel for pixel with the version before, on a desktop and on a phone screen, also with a region shown alone; the viewer's links to the guide and to the two pages; every page of both editions at a desktop and at a phone size, with every link followed, the link to the other edition and back, and the Tab key through every link; picking regions from the list in each of the four views, against the rule that the guide gives; and, in a terminal on Linux, the messages that the guide's table for when the viewer does not start describes, and `cd` with a path written the ways terminals write a dragged folder (the dragging itself was not tried). The start files did not change; the Linux start file was run only for those messages and did not serve the viewer. The other checks of the viewer were not run again, because its code did not change; the notes on the versions before, below, say what they were. Phone screens and touch were tried only in Chromium's emulation of them, and the keyboard only with key presses sent by a test program. The viewer has not yet been run on Windows, macOS or a phone.

The download contains 100 files in 64,349,634 bytes (121,479,020 bytes unpacked).

## What the version before changed, on 25 November 2026

All of it is in this version too.

The guide and the two pages about the anatomy now come in two editions, a Simple one and a Technical one. The viewer itself is as it was: the 3D model, its regions, its colours and its controls are those of the version before it, which was built earlier on the same day.

- **In the Simple edition's words.** Each of the three help pages can now be read in two ways. The Simple edition explains each step, and the words it uses where they first appear, for a reader who has never seen a brain atlas. The Technical edition says the same things more briefly, with each number beside its unit and its limit. A line at the top of every page takes you from one edition to the other, to the same section if you came to one by a link. The pages keep their addresses. `README.html` still opens the guide: it is a copy of the Simple edition, which is the file `simple.html`, and the Technical edition is `technical.html`. In the folder viewer, `source.html` and `omissions.html` are the Simple editions of the two other pages, and `source-technical.html` and `omissions-technical.html` are their Technical editions. Links that you saved still work. The viewer's own three links to the guide, and its links to the two pages, open the Simple edition; nothing else in the viewer changed. Every count in the pages is filled in from the viewer's own data when the download is built, so the two editions cannot give different counts. The download has five more files: the four new pages and the style sheet that the six pages share. To make room for them, one picture in the download, `viewer/assets/overview.png`, was packed more tightly. It holds the same pixels. Each file in the ZIP is also packed with whichever of four settings of the same method makes it smallest. This changes only how tightly the ZIP is packed, not any file in it.
- **In the Technical edition's words.** Each of the three pages is now two files, a Simple edition that explains each step and each term for a reader new to brain atlases, and a Technical edition that states the same facts more briefly, with units and limits. A line at the top of every page links the two editions and keeps the section in the address. The guide is `simple.html` and `technical.html`, and `README.html` stays as a copy of `simple.html`, byte for byte. The page on where the anatomy comes from is `viewer/source.html` (the Simple edition, at the address the page had before) and `viewer/source-technical.html`. The page on the twenty regions drawn only when asked is `viewer/omissions.html` (the Simple edition) and `viewer/omissions-technical.html`. Earlier links still work. Every count in the pages is filled in from the viewer's data files when the download is built, and the build stops if one edition states a count that the other does not. In the viewer's page the three links to the guide name `simple.html`, and its links to the two other pages open their Simple editions as before; nothing else in the viewer's page or code changed. The download has five more files: the four new pages and `editions.css`, the style sheet of the six pages. To make room for them, the picture `viewer/assets/overview.png` is packed more tightly and holds the same pixels, and each file in the ZIP is compressed with whichever of four settings of its compression method (Deflate, as in the zlib library) gives the smallest result; the unpacked files are unchanged.

The pages state the facts that the earlier pages stated, with additions and corrections. Among them:

- The guide no longer says that any part of the model can be shown on its own: 54 of its regions can.
- The four views leave out most of the white matter, not all of it. The guide gives the count of the fifteen regions that the button draws, and the page on where the anatomy comes from no longer counts all the white matter among the twenty regions drawn only when asked: some of it is part of larger surfaces.
- The parietal operculum covers only the back part of the insula from above. The pages now say so, and the page on where the anatomy comes from names the main regions that cover the insula further forward.
- The step for macOS says what to do if macOS will not open the start file, and the steps for Windows and macOS say that they have not yet been tried there. Both guides say what to do when a command typed in a terminal cannot find its file, and the Simple guide says how to move a terminal into the viewer's folder.
- The guide describes more of the controls: the walk along the ruler, **Hide the cuts**, the two stray voxels, how picking a region can change the starting view, and what the finder of the flat cuts ticks and unfolds for you. It says where the buttons for a saved view are, when **Zoom to it** can be chosen, and how a region with more than one colour group is coloured.
- The limits name the four surfaces that were not smoothed and how far points were moved at the midline, and they add the separate deep nuclei of the cerebellum, which the atlas has only together, as one region, and the working fields of the cortex, which it does not mark.
- The wait for the first view comes with the conditions under which it was timed. The view **Insula exposed** is described in the words the viewer itself uses, in place of a comparison with surgery that no source supported. The flat cuts' colours are not the colour groups of the 3D model. The responses to touch are reported for two of the four fields of the parietal operculum, as their source does. The page on the twenty regions says "almost a third" of the atlas where it said "a third".
- A command on a page breaks only between its words, and the link to the licence of Three.js opens its text.

## What the version before that changed, earlier on 25 November 2026

All of it is in this version too.

On the website the first view arrives sooner. Nothing you see changes: the 3D model, its regions and its colours are the same as in the version of 24 November 2026, and the 3D model file keeps its bytes.

- **Less to download.** Before the model shows, the website now sends about 38 megabytes instead of about 43. Through a link held to 1.6 Mbit/s, the speed of a slow phone network, the wait for the first view fell from 3 minutes 39 seconds to 3 minutes 14 seconds; at 9 Mbit/s, from about 45 seconds to 39.
- **The region files.** Ten files say which region each point of the surface belongs to. They are now named `.bin` instead of `.u32`, and their bytes are the same. The website compresses files by their type and did not compress `.u32`, so it sent 4.6 megabytes that it now sends as 0.2.
- **The small picture.** The small picture of the whole brain, and the faded picture shown while the model loads, now come from a copy of the picture that is 900 pixels wide and 25 kilobytes, instead of the original of 1800 pixels and 578 kilobytes. At the sizes the viewer draws the small picture, the two look the same.
- The original picture, `viewer/assets/overview.png`, stays in the download. It is packed more tightly and holds the same pixels.

The download starts no faster than before: its start file serves the files from your own computer.

Run for that version, on Linux in Chromium 155 with software rendering, from the download's start file and from a copy of the website served on this computer: the first view, a colour overlay, the parietal operculum shown alone, the three flat cuts, the white matter and the fluid spaces in each of the four views, three of the twenty regions that are drawn only when asked shown alone, and the page opened straight from the folder. On the copy of the website, also: the first view against the version of 24 November 2026, pixel by pixel, on a desktop and on a phone screen; the keys on the model and Tab through every control; a phone upright and sideways and a tablet; loads at 9 and at 1.6 Mbit/s; and Backslash of the Brain on this copy. Phone and tablet screens, touch and slow connections were tried only in Chromium's emulation of them, and the keyboard only with key presses sent by a test program. ## What the version of 24 November 2026 changed

All of it is in this version too.

The viewer is easier to use on a phone, from the keyboard and on a slow connection. The 3D model, its regions and its colours are the same as in the version of 21 November 2026.

- **On a phone.** On a narrow screen the 3D model stays at the top while the panel scrolls under it, so a region you show alone or zoom to stays in sight. The text and the controls are larger. In the three flat cuts, the cuts come before their explanation, and a second **Back to the 3D model** button sits at the bottom.
- **From the keyboard.** The Tab key reaches the model, and a blue ring marks it. Shift and the arrow keys turn the model, the arrow keys move it, + and − zoom.
- **On a slow connection.** While the model loads, a faded picture of the brain and a count of the megabytes show that it is arriving. A download that stops says so and offers a button that reloads the page. If some of the white matter and fluid spaces fail to load, hiding them and showing them again fetches only the ones that failed.
- **Colours.** A sentence under the colour key says what to do if two of the colours look alike to you.
- **One link.** The panel links to a separate viewer on the project's website that follows one wrist reach. That link needs the internet.

## What the version of 21 November 2026 changed

All of it is in this version too.

Twenty of the atlas's 141 regions had no surface. They were the white matter of the forebrain and five of its named bundles of nerve fibres, nine fluid spaces (the ventricles and canals), the four regions of the smell pathway and the claustrum: 2,255,724 of the atlas's 6,999,201 labelled voxels, a third of the atlas. Each now has a surface, made from the same atlas grid the same way as the thirty single-region surfaces of the main model file: a surface around the region's voxels on the half-millimetre grid, smoothed so that no point moved more than 0.125 mm.

- **Show white matter and fluid spaces**, a new button under **Reset view**, draws fifteen of them in place, in every view. The white matter of the forebrain and the fluid spaces are see-through, so the regions inside and behind them stay in sight. They are a 36 MB download, so they stay off until you ask.
- **Show it alone** works for each of the twenty, as for the other regions with a surface of their own. Its file loads when you pick it.
- Until you ask, the 3D model looks exactly as before, pixel for pixel; the panel has one new button.
- The download is larger, 64.3 MB, because it carries the new surfaces. On the website nothing more is loaded until you ask.

The notes under **Evidence and limits**, shown when you select a region, now say in plain words what the earlier ones said in working shorthand. A cited source is named by its authors and year instead of a code, each structure is explained before its abbreviation is given, and the sentence about how the sources were chosen comes once per region instead of in front of every note. Each note starts on a line of its own and says what it said before. The links below the notes go to the same sources, and each now begins with the source's authors and year instead of the project's code for it. Two of them used to give a title and year only. A small gap now separates one link from the next.

Four regions that were listed with "left atlas surface" after their names now have plain names, like every other region: ventral posterior lateral nucleus (VPL), ventral posterior medial nucleus (VPM), red nucleus and parietal operculum (PaO).

On the website the viewer now opens at [eliminatenoise.github.io/Interoceptomics/atlas](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/Interoceptomics/atlas/). The address it had until now, which ended in /viewer/, forwards there, and a link that opens a saved view keeps its view. [Backslash of the Brain](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/Interoceptomics/atlas/backslash/) sits beside it: a separate anatomy atlas that follows signals from muscles and organs through 3D models and slices of the human brain, spinal cord and organs. Its 3D brain is this viewer's 3D model, shown inside its own pages.
