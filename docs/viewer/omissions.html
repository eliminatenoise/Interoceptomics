20 regions have no surface, and together they are a third of the atlas

The 3D model holds 121 of the atlas’s 141 regions, in 44 surfaces. The other 20 are in the atlas grid and not in the 3D model: you cannot select them there, and none of them carries a colour.

They are not small. Together they hold 2,255,724 of the atlas’s 6,999,201 labelled voxels (a voxel is one half-millimetre cube of the grid), and the white matter of the forebrain alone holds 2,089,673.

Every one of them can still be read in the flat cuts. In the viewer choose Open three flat cuts, then Find a region, and type a name or a number from this table.

Why they are missing

The white matter of the forebrain was left out on purpose: drawn as a solid surface, it would wrap everything inside it. The other 19 were not among the regions chosen for a surface when the model was built. No record says that a surface could not be made for any of them.

The viewer’s list of regions keeps a proposed colour group for three of the 20 (the olfactory bulb, the anterior olfactory nucleus and the piriform cortex). It is a proposal, and nothing is drawn for it.

The four groups in the table are this project’s. The names, abbreviations and numbers are the Allen Institute’s, from its table of regions. A copy of that table travels with the viewer: sources/allen-version1/voxel_count.csv. The voxel counts were recounted from the atlas grid for this page, and they equal the counts in that table.
