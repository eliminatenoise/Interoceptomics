What this project changed in the atlas

The atlas is a grid of half-millimetre cubes, each carrying the number of a region. Its file is annotation.nii.gz. From that grid this project made the following. All of it is atlas-derived, and the credit above applies to all of it.

ChangeWhat was done
The left halfThe atlas labels almost only its left side. The 3D model keeps the cubes at R ≤ 0 in the atlas’s own right-anterior-superior coordinates: the left side and the midline.
SurfacesSurfaces were drawn around the regions on the 0.5 mm grid. The cut face at the midline was closed with a constructed cap of at most 0.25 mm. An earlier display approximation moved surfaces by at most 0.125 mm. Surface directions (normals) were set, and a plain single-sided material was applied.
GroupingSome regions were merged into combined surfaces, and thirty were given surfaces of their own in the main model file. With the four separate surfaces and the twenty more surfaces below, that makes the 54 regions the viewer can lift out.
CoordinatesPositions were converted from the atlas’s millimetres to the viewer’s metres: (R, A, S) becomes (−R, S, A) ÷ 1000.
Four separate surfacesUnsmoothed, each following one region on the same grid in the same coordinates, with display normals and a material added: the red nucleus (region 12247), the ventral posterior lateral nucleus, VPL (10424), the ventral posterior medial nucleus, VPM (10425), and the parietal operculum (13230).
Twenty more surfacesMade on 21 November 2026 by the Interoceptomics contributors, one file for each of the twenty regions that had no surface until then: the white matter of the forebrain and five named fibre bundles, nine fluid spaces, the four regions of the smell pathway and the claustrum. Each follows one region on the same grid, in the same coordinates, and was made the same way as the thirty single-region surfaces of the main model file: the left half with the same midline cap, a surface drawn around the region’s voxels, smoothed so that no point moved by more than 0.125 mm, with normals and a plain material added. The viewer shows them only when you ask.
Where VPL and VPM touchA list of the 481 grid faces between regions 10424 and 10425.
Two crops of the gridOne keeps only VPL and VPM. One keeps every full plane that contains the parietal operculum.
The whole grid, repacked without lossFor the flat cuts, the ruler and the volume counts. Every original region number is kept, in the original order, and the atlas’s own coordinate frame is recorded with it.
A lookup of starting pointsFor each region and side, the one cube of that region closest to the average position of its cubes. Ties go to the lowest index in the original order.
Additions that are not anatomyThe somatic, autonomic and integration colours, the cameras and lights, the controls, a generated overview picture, and what the viewer draws over the grid on screen: the colours of the flat cuts, highlights, marks, frames and the ruler’s line.

Two things are deliberately absent. The MRI brain template that the atlas was drawn on, the ICBM 2009b nonlinear symmetric MNI152 average, is not in the download. The atlas’s README sends you to the McConnell Brain Imaging Centre for the template and its terms of use, and gives its copyright as © 1993–2004 Louis Collins, McConnell Brain Imaging Centre, Montreal Neurological Institute, McGill University. Nothing here is a licence for it. The photographs and figures from the papers the viewer cites are not in the download either; those stay with their publishers.

The region names are the Allen Institute’s too

So are the regions’ abbreviations, their numbers and their parent-and-child order. All are covered by the same CC BY 4.0 credit, on this basis: the atlas collection publishes its own table of them, voxel_count.csv, and the collection’s README counts such tables among its materials. This download includes that table unchanged at sources/allen-version1/voxel_count.csv.

What was checked, and what was not:

  • For all 141 regions, the number, name, abbreviation and full ancestry in the viewer match the table exactly. No near-match was accepted. This was first checked on an early edition of the viewer and was checked again for this version, on 21 November 2026.
  • For all 141 regions, the table’s count of voxels equals a recount of the atlas grid in this download, made for this version. The table’s other count and volume columns, which add in each region’s sub-regions, were not checked.
  • The API response described below and the table carried the same set of region numbers and agreed exactly on name, abbreviation, ancestry, parent, order and colour, over 3,317 rows. That comparison was made once, on an early edition, and cannot be repeated from this download, because the response is not in it. Its record is at sources/allen-version1/source-binding.json. The record keeps the name and the folder the viewer had then. It names the viewer’s list of regions as it stood then, at 260,028 bytes; the list has since gained notes and display settings and is 308,840 bytes.
  • The table was downloaded on 15 November 2026. The server reports it as last modified on 19 February 2025, so it cannot be shown to be byte for byte the 2020 original.

The viewer uses the four matched fields to look a region up, to group it with its sub-regions and to name what you select. The notes, colour groups and display settings in the list of regions are this project’s additions.

The names first came through the Allen web API, whose terms are narrower

The viewer’s names and hierarchy were first fetched from the Allen Brain Map web API on 4 November 2026: structure graph 16, 3,317 rows, through this query, following the atlas’s own conversion example. It is the 2026 state of that graph, not a frozen copy from 2020. The response itself is not in the download.

The API falls under the Allen Institute’s general Terms of Use (as updated 23 October 2026), which are narrower than CC BY. They record:

  • permission for research and non-commercial use, with citation;
  • ownership staying with the Institute;
  • limits on commercial redistribution;
  • the Institute’s Freedom to Innovate conditions, which include not asserting rights against improvements the Institute develops independently, and a restriction on posting to sites that require acknowledgement of ownership of the posted content.

What that means for you:

  • The names, abbreviations, numbers and ancestry of the 141 regions match the published table, so the CC BY 4.0 credit above covers them.
  • The rest of the API response was not examined and is not in the download. This page gives no permission for it, and no blanket permission for AI use, for future improvements (the Terms’ own word is “Improvements”), or for the model as a whole.
  • That the whole response carries no licence here does not narrow CC BY 4.0 for the matching fields or for the atlas-derived files, nor the MIT licence of Three.js.
  • If you fetch from the API yourself, the Terms of Use apply to what you fetch, as they do to this project’s own fetch. Nothing here waives them.
