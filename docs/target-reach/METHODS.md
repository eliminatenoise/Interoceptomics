# Carry the cylinder

A seated person saw one location on a screen, waited for a sound, then carried a cylinder from the centre of the table to that location. Nine circles on the table stood 250 mm from the starting station. Figure 4 shows their numbering. Target 1 was on the participant’s left, 5 lay straight ahead and 9 was on the right. The two hands performed separate blocks. The optical cameras tracked the two reflective markers attached to the cylinder. The study also used an inertial sensor. The optical recording supplies the paths in this reader.

## Which recordings appear here?

The full Ruhr Hand Motion Catalog contains 31 participants. This reader takes the first assignment to each of the nine targets with each hand for the first anonymous participant in the deposited order. All 18 examples pass the authors’ optical exclusion list. This selection illustrates one person’s movements. A difference between the two traces describes these two trials. A general conclusion about handedness would require repeated observations across people. No one supplied a free-form movement command. A gesture made in response to a concept and a picture is a different experiment.

## Read the path

The 250 Hz Vicon files supply two marker positions. At the first frame with a complete marker triplet we freeze the set of available markers, take their mean at every eligible frame and subtract the initial mean. Both markers are available at the start of all 18 displayed trials. Each displacement uses that same pair throughout its trial. The source tracker may previously have filled short occlusions using the fixed relationship between markers. These files do not label every such intervention.

`records.js` includes the full selected frame grid. Its four columns hold elapsed seconds and Vicon X, Y and Z displacements in millimetres. We derive time from the native frame counter divided by 250 Hz and round each position to 0.001 mm. That decimal precision describes storage. Optical calibration and the tracking procedure determine measurement accuracy. A missing triplet would break the drawn line and hide the cylinder. All 18 selected examples have complete displayed triplets. Changing the camera rotates the full three-dimensional coordinates. The top and side buttons give flat views of the same samples.

The paths share their first usable marker sample as an origin. Target spacing and numbering come from the participant-centred apparatus diagram. The deposited camera frame runs at an angle to that arrangement. The diagram and the paths retain their own axes. The upright cylinder is a drawing keyed to the marker-centre translation. Its base measures 50 mm across and 25 mm tall. The orange housing and grey marker glyphs follow Figure 2, with approximate dimensions and pose. The grid passes through the initial marker centre, above the physical table. No arm joints, finger positions, rotation, spoken words or nerve signals were recorded in the selected optical columns.

The view opens at the selected trial’s highest Z sample. **Start** moves to the first camera sample. **First 15 mm** finds the first three consecutive native frames at least 15 mm from the origin in three dimensions and moves to the third frame. That operational threshold helps locate movement amid several seconds of preparation. The camera clock in these excerpts does not establish the instant of the target display or the go sound. A second path stays in view in full while the slider moves the first recording’s cylinder. The open endpoint circle marks the last measured position. Measuring error against a printed target centre would require registering the physical targets to the camera frame.

## Use the files

Extract the entire offline reader. Double-click **Open on Windows.cmd** on Windows or **Open on macOS.command** on macOS. On Linux, run `sh "Open on Linux.sh"`. Opening `index.html` in a recent browser also works. Drag the scene or focus it and press the arrow keys. Use the wheel or plus/minus keys to zoom. The slider and sample buttons step through the original clock. External study links need an internet connection.

Each of the 18 `.glb` files in `models/` opens independently. They store metres in glTF’s right-handed Y-up frame. A Vicon point `(x,y,z)` in millimetres becomes `(x,z,−y)/1000`. A STEP animation holds each observed sample until the next. The static path connects consecutively observed points. The model’s local origin lies between its two illustrated optical markers. The exported meshes include no reconstructed person or measured rotation. Rebuild all 18 models with `python export-glb.py` (Python 3.10 or newer, standard library).

The source record includes each selected CSV path and SHA-256 digest. To verify the points yourself, extract **Ruhr_HandMotion_CenterOutTask.tar.gz**, version 1. Its SHA-256 is `ebcde74b260f4a1e7b57ce0bb626bb6ee0703e6ba75c258267211933da3d8c79`. Run `python check-source.py /path/to/folder-containing-Output`. The check reads the original first target assignments, optical exclusions, marker membership, frame clock, positions and displacement threshold. It compares every displayed coordinate to the source within half the 0.001 mm rounding increment. [Original archive](https://osf.io/download/4tq78/?version=1)
