When the pedal holds still

Try to lift a foot strapped to a fixed pedal. Skin electrodes pick up electrical activity from the muscle beside the shinbone. A separate sensor under the pedal records the load. What changes in the next tenth of a second?

How much turning force?

A load applied away from a pivot produces a turning effect called a moment. The study’s platform used a 0.100 m moment arm. Put an example force into the calculation and see how much external moment it would produce.

The example force is yours to choose. A participant’s muscle force cannot be recovered from this calculation. Several muscles and passive tissues can contribute to a joint’s response.

Read the measurements

Two people took part in the eight recordings here. Each tried to raise the forefoot against a fixed pedal at two settings, twice at each setting. The board recorded the conditioned muscle and load-cell outputs together at a nominal 1,000 samples per second. The plotted time scale is nominal. The last 200 sample intervals, nominally 200 ms, cover 201 samples including both endpoints. At reading time t, the target is the recorded load 100 rows later (nominally 100 ms) minus the load at t.

The source names its two selected settings angle0 and angle5. Angle5 and angle_5 name different archive records. The sign and direction of the five-degree setting remain unresolved. The muscle graph ends at t. On the load graph, the shaded section starts at t and contains the actual later readings when revealed. This is a replay. The data establish no physiological delay, and the load channel called “right” identifies a channel on the equipment. Both plots connect original samples in source order.

The published methods describe a weight calibration. The measured gain, its sign and the trial-specific conversion to force remain unavailable here. The 3D model locates the muscle and the sensor with a neutral reference pose across all selections.
