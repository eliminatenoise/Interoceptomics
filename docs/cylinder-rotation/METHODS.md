# How the cylinder turns

A seated participant carried a 41 g object from a starting station to one of nine circles on a table. The printed cylinder measured 50 mm across and 25 mm tall. Latches held an inertial sensor on top. Two reflective markers let the laboratory cameras track the same object. Figure 2 shows the hardware. Figure 4 locates the targets. The screen, sound and hand each had their own part to play. Figure 6 shows the experimental timing. ## Read the instrument

Choose a hand and target to open one original recording. The two hands performed separate trials. Each starts at the first exported inertial sample. The study does not establish where the target display or the sound fell on that clock within the recording. The default view picks the fastest measured turn. Press **Play** to run from there, or move the slider to zero for the whole trial. **−10 ms** and **+10 ms** step through single samples. Clicking the trace selects a sample. Drag the model to orbit, or focus the scene and use the arrow keys. **Reset view** restores the camera angle.

The reader contains the earliest usable training recording for each hand and target, in participant and recording order. All eighteen examples come from one anonymous participant. The selected files passed the original optical and inertial quality checks, had finite sensor channels, a complete exported 100 Hz grid and quaternions within 0.001 of unit length. The gallery contains 6,421 native rows. Its time grid has no packet counter or independent arrival timestamps. Onboard calibration and filtering can affect the signals. A regular export cannot establish the internal processing delay or rule out a filled packet.

The gyroscope reports angular velocity about the device's own axes in radians per second. The accelerometer reports acceleration in metres per second squared, including gravity. The chart shows the three signed sensor components. The 3D arrow uses their direction and magnitude in the illustrated body frame. Orange uses 8 mm of model space per rad/s. Turquoise uses 3.2 mm per m/s². The corner key follows the device's X, Y and Z axes. The object's centre stays fixed. These recordings cannot identify a position by double integration, a joint rotation, a muscle contraction or nerve activity. [Instrument manual, §§11.6–11.7](https://www.xsens.com/hubfs/Downloads/Manuals/MTw_Awinda_User_Manual.pdf)

## Which way did it turn?

The four source components are scalar-first unit quaternions (w, x, y, z). Each is a firmware estimate taking device axes into the local earth reference. Let q₀ be the first orientation and qₜ the current one. The displayed relative rotation is q₀⁻¹ qₜ. It maps the current device frame into the first device frame. All three axes initially coincide with that reference. The sensor's mounting yaw on the cylinder is illustrative. The recordings do not register the sensor heading to target 5, the camera frame or an anatomical plane.

The right-handed display and glTF use Y up. A native reference vector (x, y, z) maps to (x, z, −y). For a normalized relative quaternion (w, x, y, z), glTF therefore stores (x, z, −y, w). Consecutive export keys choose the same sign hemisphere. Each animation holds a pose until the next native sample. The housing and marker dimensions approximate the study photograph. A change in camera angle only changes the view. No wrist, hand, optical trajectory or body translation drives the scene.

## Forecast and observation

For each of seven cutoffs, the 20 preceding readings form five four-sample intervals. The full model sees gyroscope and accelerometer readings, elapsed time, hand and assigned target. Its output predicts mean angular velocity on the three sensor axes over the following ten recorded samples. The comparison uses the fixed full transformer fitted with seed 17. Changing the cutoff moves playback to the start of the outcome. The tabulated result stays on that window while the playback cursor moves. The three-axis error is the Euclidean distance between the measured and predicted vectors in rad/s.

The gallery examples were part of model fitting. A separate test used later repetitions from 24 people already represented in fitting. On 2,653 test windows with a matching earlier repetition, the full model missed by 0.1584 rad/s, averaged across three fits and with each person weighted equally. Removing the destination gave 0.1508 rad/s. The full model’s error minus the gyroscope-and-task model’s error was −0.0038 rad/s, with a paired 95% participant interval from −0.0080 to +0.0005 rad/s. Those data leave the improvement from adding acceleration to that model uncertain. The number in the atlas table describes one particular recording, window and fit. Projecting the full model’s mean rate forward gave a 0.909° mean orientation error on these same matched test windows. Holding the latest pose gave 1.185°. The reference at the endpoint is the device’s own orientation estimate. ## Files and verification

Extract the whole ZIP. Open the launcher for your operating system or open index.html directly in a modern browser. On Linux, you can run `sh "Open on Linux.sh"`. The replay, illustrated apparatus, forecast comparisons, figures and eighteen GLB animations work offline. Source links and the companion model website use the internet. A remote link to the complete ZIP remains available from the reader.

Each entry in recordings.js names the original measurement-X member and its SHA-256. Its rows hold elapsed seconds, three acceleration values, three gyroscope values and four orientation values. The full source measurements retain their exported precision. Its windows include each cutoff, ten-row measured mean and fitted three-axis forecast. The entry for the seed-17 model gives its weights hash. To check an extracted dataset against the gallery, run `python check-recordings.py /path/to/Output`. Python 3.10 or later suffices. Rebuild the eighteen animations with `python export-models.py`.

The numerical source is version 1 of Ruhr_HandMotion_CenterOutTask.tar.gz, SHA-256 `ebcde74b260f4a1e7b57ce0bb626bb6ee0703e6ba75c258267211933da3d8c79`. The public model companion carries the forecasting methods and fitted weights.
