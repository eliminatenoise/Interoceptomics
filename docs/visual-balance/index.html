Read the measurements and sources

One turn of a virtual room

Peterson and Ferris recorded a walker on a treadmill-mounted beam. The visual scene rotated 20° for half a second. The log places this turn between stored samples 10485 and 10486. The button for visual onset selects sample 10486, 1.68 ms after the log. The clock measures elapsed time from the fractional onset. It does not measure display latency or prove when the walker perceived the turn.

Scalp, eyes and muscles

The scalp contacts record electrical potential relative to the authors’ common median reference. Their stored EEG, eye and neck recordings were high-pass filtered at 1 Hz and treated for line noise. Raw optical tracking ran at 100 Hz and leg muscle recording at 1,000 Hz. The authors synchronized and resampled these streams to the stored 256 Hz clock. We added no filtering, source localization or signal interpolation. These scalp potentials can include movement and muscle signals. They cannot tell us which brain network produced a voltage.

How the geometry travels

The researchers digitized each person’s electrode locations. The SET file retains more digits than the table, and its coordinates agree with the table within 0.005 m per axis. The coordinate sidecar names CTF/EEGLAB and gives millimetres, while the authors’ data paper explicitly gives metres for this table. This reader uses the paper’s units and the SET coordinates. There is no anatomical mesh. The electrode and motion frames are displayed separately.

For movement, ML means mediolateral, SI superior–inferior and AP anterior–posterior. The numbers keep the original signs. The source leaves anatomical positive directions and the alignment between head and laboratory frames unresolved. Motion at the two points gives no joint angle or measure of total-body sway.

Independent things you can inspect

  • Compare the 3D digitizer locations with the published electrode table and the recorded scalp readings with the researchers’ original binary samples. The authors mark 113 of 128 scalp channels usable in this recording.
  • Read the log, the first sample after onset, the observation cutoff at 10518 and the later movement at 10550. Cutoff to endpoint is exactly 32 stored intervals, or 125 ms.
  • Check the study’s equipment and electrode placement in Figure 1 of the data paper. A second study paper describes how the digitizer and reflective markers were used.
