How the comparison works

We took the centre second of the cue and the centre second of the following blank interval in each of 88 complete trial pairs from one person. For each contact we compared the power in a chosen frequency band in those two seconds. A high value describes a change in recorded power. The time controls repeat the comparison in seven half-second windows that start 200 ms apart and overlap by 300 ms. The glove values average the size of changes between successive 40 ms readings within those same windows. The recording alone cannot tell us what this person felt, or which site caused a finger to bend.

Measurements and recording limits

There were 18 thumb, 19 index, 22 middle, 16 ring and 13 little-finger cues. Forty-two contacts have a documented regional label. Four retain their recording numbers but have no named region. Two other regions in the study codebook had no contacts here. The spread reported for each area is the middle half of the trial changes. It describes variation in this recording.

The energy in each band was estimated from the original 1,000-sample-per-second cortical recording. The skin of the hand, peripheral nerves and spinal cord were not recorded in this comparison. A separate glove recorded finger bending. The original report measured an offset between the glove and the electrical amplifier. Explore the glove study

Original study, anatomy and reuse

Kai Joshua Miller and colleagues collected the FingerFlex recordings. The source and these derived summaries carry the Creative Commons Attribution–ShareAlike 4.0 licence. We selected one recorded segment, paired cue and blank intervals, calculated changes in band power and joined channel numbers to the source anatomy. Use of the source requires respect for participants’ privacy and confidentiality. Project code is dedicated under CC0.
