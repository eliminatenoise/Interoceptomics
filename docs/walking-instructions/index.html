How much ground?

Forward travel of the pelvis since the selected foot struck the ground. The four pelvis markers supply its centre. Over one complete right stride the pelvis travelled 1.32 m in 0.99 s on the usual walk and 1.70 m in 0.76 s on the fast walk.

Muscle activity on the right

Eight recording channels followed muscles in the right leg on both walks. Choose a muscle to compare the electrical activity recorded at the skin.

Which foot landed on a plate?

The floor plates measured upward load. A foot needed a reliable plate assignment, with its heel and forefoot inside the plate at strike and 100 milliseconds later. These two walks qualify on opposite feet, so there is no same-foot force comparison.

Where the measurements come from

Céline Schreiber and Florent Moissenet recorded barefoot walks on a 10 m walkway. These are the first C4 and C5 recordings from one participant in their Rehazenter Adult Walking Dataset, version 8. The words come from the study protocol. There is no audio or timestamp for a spoken cue. Read the study.

The cameras tracked 52 skin markers on the feet, legs, pelvis, trunk, shoulders, arms and hands. The grey forms connect measured sites and give the limbs width. The head was unmarked. Earlier processing filled gaps and filtered the signals in both directions. These walks describe this person on two passes. Get the source recordings.

In the heel readout, F means forward, L means left and Z means up. Travel coordinates turn the horizontal displacement of the pelvis between successive right strikes towards positive forward. The starting pelvis supplies the horizontal origin. Laboratory coordinates retain the original axes and positions. The usual walk went towards negative laboratory X and the fast walk towards positive X. Each camera follows its walker at a common scale. Grid spacing is 25 cm. The heel trace covers the selected foot’s complete stride.

The optical frame number begins at 1. Its exported time is (frame − 1) ÷ 100 seconds. Each muscle reading is the root mean square voltage of the corresponding 15 samples at 1,500 Hz, expressed in microvolts. These electrodes measure electrical activity near the muscle. They supply no direct recording from the brain or nerves.
