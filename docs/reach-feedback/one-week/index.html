Two days on the same apparatus

Chowdhury, Glaser and Miller recorded rhesus macaque C reaching to targets in two workspaces. The models learned from the near workspace on 7 September. The test uses the near and far workspaces from two recording blocks on 14 September. Each replay is the first eligible original reach in its block and workspace, selected by controller timing before reading its measurements.

The processed recordings share a 10 ms clock. Optical interpolation, filtering and offline spike sorting limit real-time interpretation. The export gives a spike count in each bin; it supplies neither a voltage trace nor the instant of each spike. These recordings cannot establish which peripheral nerves produced a response or whether a movement caused it.
