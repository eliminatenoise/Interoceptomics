# After the reach

Raeed Chowdhury, Joshua Glaser and Lee Miller recorded a rhesus macaque moving a planar robot handle to successive visual targets. A camera followed painted arm landmarks. An electrode array in area 2 of primary somatosensory cortex recorded electrical spikes. The authors released the processed measurements under CC0. This atlas replays selected windows from animal C on 7 September 2017. [Dryad recording and citation](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.nk98sf7q7) · [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.48198) · [CC0 legal code](CC0-1.0.txt)

## Two frames, one clock

The optical positions are the stored XYZ coordinates of Marker_1 on the hand and Pronation_Pt1 on the proximal ulna near the elbow. They are shoulder-relative metres. The latter marker approximates elbow position. Painted markers, lighting, optical reconstruction, centered filtering, interpolation across missing samples and shoulder registration limit their accuracy. The atlas draws spheres to mark measured positions. A dotted straight guide joins the two sites. The radius of a sphere, the guide and the plotting grid are display geometry. The plot can turn around all three recorded coordinates. Each orthographic preset names the two displayed source axes. The glTF maps source (X,Y,Z) metres to (X,Z,−Y) metres. A separate encoder plot shows the handle in the apparatus XY frame in centimetres. Its glTF maps (X,Y) centimetres to (X/100,0,−Y/100) metres. The files preserve separate origins. [Authors’ marker definitions](https://github.com/raeedcho/s1-kinematics/blob/e6363cf724d88467d06d472563221a9c85d4dd1b/lib/lib-two-workspace/mwEncoders.m) · [Marker processing](https://github.com/limblab/KinectTracking/blob/f656ab81dd0ea2072c642f02afbe8f8fc472c3db/functions/transformForOpenSim.m) · [Trial-data conversion](https://github.com/raeedcho/loadTDfromCDS/blob/079e6a9ff70ef5498894d32753a13d58997b5702/processCDS.m)

The recording member is `reaching_experiments/C_20170907_TRT_TD.mat`. Its SHA-256 is `6062b7e8dacdf46f7eee465bd844ffe1fb3b7ce0793c34d3e4d106be357f4746`. All times refer to its processed 10 ms grid. Bins start at the row shown in the reader. Sorting and filtering used information unavailable to a real-time device. The study provides neither raw camera exposure times for these rows nor absolute calibration error. A unit is a positive electrode-channel/sort-number pair, in the order of the source guide. An integer count says how many spikes fell in a bin. Neither a sub-bin spike time nor a precise neuron location can be recovered from it. [Source archive](https://zenodo.org/records/5010943/files/s1-kinematics.zip) · [Spike binning](https://github.com/limblab/TrialData/blob/83c2d680e9f1122b7f29e5bd171b705e5b4a18df/DataProcessing/convertDataToTD.m)

Workspace 1 is near the animal and contralateral to the reaching arm. Workspace 2 is farther away and ipsilateral. Both occur on the same recording day. Workspace 2 trials were interleaved with workspace 1 trials. The source’s timed go words identify successive controller events. They cannot identify a target’s exact physical position or reveal the animal’s intended path. The forecast cutoff k is five bins after a second, third or fourth go within the original trial. Each replay was preselected by source order, using the first metadata-eligible window in its split. An excluded fixed window is reported with its original reason. The clip selector names the split and original identity. [Authors’ workspace analysis](https://github.com/raeedcho/s1-kinematics/blob/e6363cf724d88467d06d472563221a9c85d4dd1b/twoworkspace_analysis.m) · [Controller conversion](https://github.com/raeedcho/preprocess-from-limblab/blob/d7b78757bdfdd82cbc99aeeec549c763cc76ad17/convertTRTfiles.m)

## Read the forecast

| MATLAB rows, inclusive | Time relative to k | What the reader shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| k−20…k−1 | [−200,0) ms | Earlier tracked positions, encoder coordinates and spike counts. Models also received elapsed go-cue history up to each input bin. |
| k…k+9 | [0,100) ms | Ten position samples at 0…90 ms compared with forecasts made at k. The 100-ms endpoint is measured from the last known sample at −10 ms. Same-bin spikes here are descriptive. |
| k+10…k+19 | [100,200) ms | Ten later observed spike counts per unit compared with fitted expected counts. The optical/encoder paths end at +90 ms. |

At cutoff the full motor Transformer takes earlier poses, spike counts and recorded go words and predicts ten displacements per coordinate. Add the last available position to get each forecast in its own physical frame. Hand and elbow errors are Euclidean distances in millimetres. Handle errors use centimetres. The count comparison uses two Transformer estimates from the same earlier information. One takes a forecast of the next ten movement samples as an additional input. Model training uses predicted movements from earlier training blocks for this extra input, where they exist. The earliest training block supplied no such predictions. Its example can show an in-sample motor fit and has no fitted later-count estimate. Validation and assessment forecasts come from models fitted on training originals. Displayed expectations average three fits with seeds 17, 41 and 73. The study’s inferential comparisons average seed-wise losses. A separate control substitutes a movement forecast from an earlier original in the same split, block and go ordinal. Each fixed example occurs before such a donor is available. The linked study reports this comparison over its matched cohort. Summing a unit's expectations yields a count over the ten bins. These expectations may be fractional. A time-ordered predictive relationship in an offline replay does not establish sensory causality or a closed-loop intervention. [Forecasting study and executable models](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/reach-feedback/) · [Study paper and held comparisons](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/papers/reach-feedback/)

The secondary comparison uses a shared encoder for motion and later counts. One recurrent decoder rolls its own predicted displacement and hidden state into its next step. A matched parallel model predicts each step from the earlier context. Neither receives measured future movement. The reader overlays the rollout's motion forecast and compares both count estimates. These joint models trained on originals in training pieces 1–4. The initial piece, which contains the selected training example, stayed outside that fit.

## Visual sources and rights

Figures 1, 2 and 3 come from *Area 2 of primary somatosensory cortex encodes kinematics of the whole arm*, eLife 9:e48198, by Chowdhury, Glaser and Miller (2020), licensed CC BY 4.0. Each complete figure was resized preserving aspect ratio and converted to WebP. Figure 1 locates implanted arrays and receptive-field mapping sessions. Figure 2 shows the marker camera and robot task. Figure 3 shows the two physical workspaces and an example neural raster from the published study. These reference illustrations guide interpretation of the apparatus and site. They do not supply coordinates for the selected clips. The original dataset and this repository's new code are CC0. The study authors did not make the added motor or count forecasts. [Original figures](https://elifesciences.org/articles/48198/figures) · [Figure license](CC-BY-4.0.txt)

The selected arrays and forecasts are stored in `recordings.js`. A JSON object supplies each clip's 20 earlier poses and counts, ten native movement displacements, ten concurrent and ten later spike-count rows, the unit guide and available fitted model outputs. The four integer keys (block, original trial, go ordinal, k) join forecasts to recordings. Use the study's example JSON and fitted NPZ files as inputs to `build-clips.py` (Python 3 with NumPy). Its CLI emits the JSON for that example, with the workspace supplied as 1 or 2. `export-models.py` rebuilds all GLBs from this atlas using Python's standard library.
