# The bump and the reach

Chowdhury RH, Glaser JI and Miller LE (2020) published the macaque data and the experimental account. This atlas follows original trial 499 of animal C on 12 September 2017. The public data member is `reaching_experiments/C_20170912_COactpas_TD.mat`. Its SHA256 is `a391516945a67aff89039f33c0829a1c5423d859ce79baaaca962985916996af`. The authors released the dataset under CC0 1.0. [Dryad dataset](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.nk98sf7q7) · [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.48198) · [Dataset license](CC0-1.0.txt)

## Where the measurements come from

- **Markers.** Eight numbered sites, a proximal-ulna point and the shoulder centre share a shoulder-relative XYZ frame in metres. Their labels come directly from the MAT. In the authors’ arm model, markers 1–3 belong to the hand, 4 and 5 to the radius, 6 to the ulna, and 7 and 8 to the humerus. The active/passive analysis uses Marker_3 as its hand proxy and Pronation_Pt1 as an elbow proxy. The latter differs from the anatomical elbow joint centre. Shoulder_JC defines the origin. Dots reproduce the processed positions. Guide lines join groups. The optional body outline interpolates between groups with illustrative widths. Its surface, finger outlines and individual joint centres were never measured here. [Arm model and marker sites](https://github.com/limblab/monkeyArmModel/blob/d7667011273e3ded63f14ff465123816628acb41/monkeyArm_current.osim) · [Active/passive analysis](https://github.com/raeedcho/s1-kinematics/blob/e6363cf724d88467d06d472563221a9c85d4dd1b/lib/lib-active-passive/actpasSep.m)
- **Processing.** The lab’s software aligns marker timing and space with the apparatus, filters positions, removes the shoulder offset and converts centimetres to metres. The trial-data converter reorders axes and interpolates onto the stored clock. The view labels the final MAT axes X, Y and Z. The top, side and front controls describe projections in that frame. This file lacks the raw frames, visibility records and exact calibration error. The 10-ms interval is the released analysis grid. [Kinect marker pipeline](https://github.com/limblab/ClassyDataAnalysis/blob/3d23730956b05aa19594cbfc6dcb2b4ab8833f34/%40commonDataStructure/loadRawMarkerData.m) · [Shoulder transformation](https://github.com/limblab/KinectTracking/blob/f656ab81dd0ea2072c642f02afbe8f8fc472c3db/functions/transformForOpenSim.m) · [Trial-data conversion](https://github.com/raeedcho/loadTDfromCDS/blob/079e6a9ff70ef5498894d32753a13d58997b5702/processCDS.m)
- **Robot handle and cues.** Robot encoders give `pos` in planar centimetres. The absolute MATLAB row numbers are 147193–147442 inclusive. Perturbation starts at 147356. Target and go both occur at 147387. The robot perturbation and the visual target each have direction 180° in this trial. The paper describes a macaque moving a handle that controls a cursor. The go time marks the cue, distinct from a measured movement onset. The recorded arrays cannot tell us what the animal thought or supply a spoken instruction. [Encoder calculation](https://github.com/limblab/ClassyDataAnalysis/blob/3d23730956b05aa19594cbfc6dcb2b4ab8833f34/%40commonDataStructure/kinematicsFromNEV.m) · [Date-specific handling of the paired epochs](https://github.com/raeedcho/s1-kinematics/blob/e6363cf724d88467d06d472563221a9c85d4dd1b/actpas_analysis.m)
- **Cortical spikes.** Each displayed unit has a positive sorting number in the session’s S1 unit guide. Their 571 counts in this trial occupy 250 bins × 30 columns. They describe sorted electrical events in area 2 of primary somatosensory cortex. Exact neuron coordinates and raw waveforms are unavailable. The display never assigns a spike count to an arm marker. [Spike-count loader](https://github.com/raeedcho/loadTDfromCDS/blob/079e6a9ff70ef5498894d32753a13d58997b5702/processCDSspikes.m)

## Replaying the forecast

The training, validation and final test blocks contain 324, 108 and 108 chronological rewarded original trials from this one session. The case was fixed as the first rewarded original in the final block before scoring. All eight eligible forecasts in this case are accessible. At row k, the output is the next planar encoder displacement, pos[k+10] − pos[k]. Five consecutive 40-ms input bins end at k−16, k−12, k−8, k−4 and k. Each bin uses its endpoint position and the change since the previous endpoint, 30 sums of spike counts over the four completed rows, and the flags and directions for events strictly earlier than that endpoint. The current spike row k and the optical markers never enter the joint Transformer. The browser uses all three saved joint fits on these inputs. It adds each forecast to the current encoder position only on the encoder plot. The trial’s other spikes and marker coordinates remain available for retrospective replay. [Study and model](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/cortical-reach/) · [Paper](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/papers/cortical-reach/)

`trial.js` contains the selected case’s original labels, positions and counts in native order. `weights.js` carries the three fitted weight sets. `predict.js` runs the same session-bound Transformer as the companion NeuroTranslation study. `evaluate.js` rebuilds the causal histories from this trial. `forecasts.js` records the eight anchor identities and the held comparison. The GLB contains all ten marker positions per stored frame, with thin guides and a STEP animation. Its right-handed Y-up convention maps source (X, Y, Z) to glTF (X, Z, −Y) in metres. Run `python export-glb.py` from the extracted folder to rebuild the file. The browser rotates original XYZ directly.
