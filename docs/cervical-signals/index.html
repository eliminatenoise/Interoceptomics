Where the voltages and surfaces come from

Nierula and colleagues stimulated the left median nerve at the wrist while participants lay still. Sites shared a 10,000 Hz clock. Each voltage is a difference between two electrodes. The sites used different reference electrodes, so their signs and amplitudes need that context. Study and methods · Original montage.

This reader takes the first 80 median stimuli in one run from each of four training participants in OpenNeuro ds004388 v1.0.0. It shows native samples every 0.1 ms in three intervals, each including its start and excluding its end. The acquisition hardware filtered the signal. We added no filter, artifact subtraction or sign change. The baseline is each event’s mean from −15 to <−10 ms. Switch to the original reference to see the recorded levels before subtraction. We average the events within each person, then give each person equal weight. The group band spans the four person means at each sample. The gap around the stimulus contains no samples in this reader. Other electrical sources, including the heartbeat and the stimulus itself, can enter a surface recording. Four training participants cannot establish where a voltage originated or how it travelled. The authors’ processed group responses used many more trials and dedicated artifact correction. This experiment supplies no joint trajectory or force measurement. Published cervical results · Study of cardiac contamination.

Reference anatomy comes from the Human Reference Atlas, the FieldTrip standard scalp and a schematic arm. The reference bodies and the participants are different people.
