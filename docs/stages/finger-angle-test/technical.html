Four-finger displacement at 320 milliseconds

The target was signed change in metacarpophalangeal angle, the bend at the base knuckle, from issue frame t to t + 8. The index, middle, ring and little fingers were tracked. The thumb was uninstrumented. A future absolute angle would also require its starting angle. The results reader reports mean absolute error in degrees.

Signals and test design

The authors synchronized forearm ultrasound at 25 Hz with finger angles derived from a 100 Hz, ten-camera marker system. The analysis used 101 samples through the issue frame, a four-second span. Forty recordings from four participants supplied the fit. The two previously excluded participants contributed 20 recordings. Index flexion used medium speed. Ring flexion, index-to-thumb pinch and hook each used slow, medium and fast speeds. Participants repeated movements to a beat.

All 26,000 issue times were sealed before scoring against future optical measurements. The final eight issues in each of the 20 recordings had no later measurement, leaving 25,840 common scored times. Windows overlap. The independent test units are the two participants.

Methods and aggregation

Hold predicts zero change. Trend extrapolates a least-squares line through the latest five angles. Replay finds a lag independently for each finger from eight to 75 frames. It minimizes the mean squared difference in the ten-frame angle template plus half the mean squared difference in successive angle changes. A tie takes the shorter lag. The forecast copies the earlier matched angle eight frames later and subtracts the angle at the current issue to obtain its signed change. Adaptive replay chooses between replay and hold using errors at completed prior issues.

The three linear and three recurrent models used angle history, image history, or both. On a four-by-four grid, each feature was log(1 + mean restored echo amplitude) in its region. The first frame’s feature was subtracted from later values in the same region. Input scaling came from the fitting people. Ridge penalty was 0.01. The recurrent models used width 24, three passes and 160 updates of 64 windows. Predictions were averaged across two training seeds. Ultrasound-only inputs estimated angle change without past measured angles.

Take absolute error per finger and issue. Average issues within recording and the selected fingers. Within each participant give equal weight to speeds within gesture, then to gestures. Average the two participant values. A speed can be selected after choosing a gesture. In the all-finger, all-condition view the primary replay-minus-hold differences were −9.98° and −6.62° for A and B.

Overall replay and hold were 5.46° and 13.76°, respectively, a 60.3% reduction. A had 5.38° versus 15.36° and B had 5.54° versus 12.16°. For index flexion the pair was 6.66° versus 25.02°. For hook it was 3.21° versus 3.81°. Ultrasound-only recurrence had 16.01° error. This comparison supports an angle-history baseline in this repeated task and recording setup. Spontaneous motion, clinical performance, equipment transfer and live acquisition latency were outside the test.
