What changes when the model makes another pass

The model reads the last fifth of a second of muscle voltages and vertical force underfoot. It has 24 layers that process these measurements. The middle 12 can repeat. With three passes it goes through that middle section three times, using the output from the previous pass. The weights were fitted at three passes and stayed fixed for all five settings.

The first row is a simple comparison. It assumes the current force stays steady and uses the chance of a new load measured among the training walks. The force score measures the average size of the mistake in newton-seconds. The probability score squares the difference between the prediction and what actually happened.

Read the comparison

Each of three walkers counts equally. Their nine walks supplied 432 rows for force and 427 for new loading. Eighteen of the 427 rows show a new load. They cover eight separate events because neighbouring forecasts can capture the same event. Choose Use the same rows for both errors to restrict both scores to those 427 rows.

With all usable force rows, three passes had the lowest force error, 2.895 newton-seconds. The comparison that kept the last force gave 3.237. On the new-load score, five passes gave 0.038593 and three gave 0.038625. This small difference applies to the previously scored validation walks used in this comparison. Three of six assigned walkers had the required recordings. The final test group stayed unopened.
