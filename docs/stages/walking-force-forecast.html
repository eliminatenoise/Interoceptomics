Walking forecasts with measured force

Cameras tracked markers on the heels of 16 people walking with an above-knee prosthesis at 0.6 m/s. Force plates measured how hard each foot pressed down. The models used 200 milliseconds of earlier readings to forecast the next 100 milliseconds.

Adding force made the impulse forecast more accurate for all 16 walkers.

Vertical impulse is the force under a foot added up over the forecast interval. Average error fell from 16.35 newton-seconds using heel positions alone to 7.13 using heel positions and force. For each input choice, the comparison averaged the forecasts of two training runs.

These sensors measured heel movement and floor force. Muscle electrical activity would need a separate recording. To learn what walking felt like, we would need the walkers’ own reports.
