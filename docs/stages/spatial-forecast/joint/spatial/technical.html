Spatial movement prediction at 120 ms

Five past XYZ poses feed a compact transformer that forecasts twelve bilateral shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle sites. Optional right-leg surface EMG provides eight RMS voltages per time step. The viewer draws measured optical markers, the forecast and, on reveal, the measured future positions. Choose a point, a time, a coordinate frame and a method. Rotation is manual. Open the 3D viewer · Open NeuroSurge · Download the portable study

Run the model

Extract the portable ZIP. Node.js 18 or later runs its desktop launchers. In the extracted directory node run-example.mjs runs the paired example and node run-example.mjs recording.json runs another input. Linux and Chromium were exercised. Windows and macOS launchers are supplied. Use the viewer's file input for a local recording. In NeuroSurge Full or Lite, press F, enter forecast in 3D, and press Enter. Alternatively press C, choose Forecast a tracked point and expand 3D motion forecast. Choose a point. Select Start camera, a local video or timestamped stills. The single-camera panel defaults to the last estimate and also offers the trained model.

A spatial input has unit: "mm" and frames. Supply five chronological samples with seconds in t and 12 XYZ triples in xyz. Use null for missing points. No consecutive interval may exceed 240 ms. The final point and at least three observations of it are required for a learned estimate, with at least three visible final sites. Missing current points are withheld. A visible unsupported point uses persistence. Set reference to lab or hips. Hip-centred estimates require both hips. Optional rms_volts has eight nonnegative entries in the documented channel order. Enable it with use_muscles.

A paired input supplies two views, their matching frames and per-camera calibration. Use 12 named points or all 33 MediaPipe landmarks in xy; the latter matches the image benchmark. Both views must observe the same person and timestamps, within one microsecond. Row-vector projection rotates the world point, adds translation in mm, applies two radial lens coefficients and uses a row-vector intrinsic matrix. The working pixels are (native distorted pixels minus pixel_origin) times pixel_scale. The reader inverts radial distortion and takes the midpoint of the shortest segment between the forward rays. It requires positive depths, a ray angle at least 2° from parallel or antiparallel, and a maximum reprojection error of 12 px. Unusable future geometry falls back to the current estimate. The example pair is simulated from measured CMU positions with two illustrative cameras.

In the lab frame, paired input defaults to Two-camera tracking feedback. It uses the published 0.0 image forecast, recent image-trajectory estimates and the compact human image model, with weights updated from resolved tracker errors. Each view keeps its own history and per-joint issue clock. CPU tracking and the model run locally. Single-camera 3D coordinates use the pose tracker's hip-relative estimate in metres converted to mm, with the observed hip midpoint subtracted again. Its displayed score uses a later tracker estimate.

Checkpoint and reproduction

The portable model folder contains the FP32 spatial checkpoint, architecture, NumPy geometry, Python inference and a local fine-tuning command. In each labelled JSONL row supply the same frames, a disjoint participant group, a point index and its reference endpoint 120 ms later. model/research reconstructs the source arrays and refits the released checkpoint from the preceding image weights. A fresh execution reproduced all six source fit/tune archives, the tuning trace, the linear coefficients and the selected checkpoint byte for byte on this machine. On 1,861 finite joint outputs, JavaScript differed from PyTorch by at most 0.00033 mm. On 3,425 matching paired image-feedback forecasts, the maximum difference was 0.000065 mm.

Model and source split

The original human image checkpoint initializes two width-32, four-head temporal attention blocks. Five samples encode norms and pairwise dot products of normalized joint, neighbour, contralateral and shared-body motion vectors, observation masks, relative times and joint class. Fixed ridge coefficients and learned coefficients combine the vectors. The construction preserves rotation and reflection of the coordinate axes and uniform scale. An optional 17-input projection adds eight log-transformed RMS values, eight observation flags and the measured-side indicator to the temporal representation. The compact model has 18,532 trainable parameters and 120 fixed coefficients. All training operations used FP32.

CMU retained 64 fitting, 17 tuning and nine source-check groups. Source codes need not identify unique people. Georgia used five fitting, one tuning and four source-check participants with the left shoulder and bilateral hip, knee and ankle sites. Schreiber and Moissenet contributed 685 five-speed recordings from 24 fitting and six tuning people, with ten further test participants. The normalized loss weighted laboratories, groups, conditions, clips and joint classes. Source perturbations included correlated coordinate noise, static site offsets and missing points. EMG used the previous 30 ms RMS, fitted channel scales and full-channel dropout on half the eligible fitting draws. From the first channel through the eighth, the right-leg signals come from the tibialis anterior, soleus, medial gastrocnemius, vastus medialis, rectus femoris, semitendinosus, gluteus maximus and gluteus medius. Candidate selection used only the 73,634 tuning examples. Fitting used 311,368 examples, 128 per update. The best checkpoint was at 9,000 of 9,500 updates, after four checks without a 0.5% improvement against the last substantial best.

Calibrated camera benchmark

Two fixed MoVi cameras recorded 30 Hz images, synchronized with 120 Hz optical reference positions. Both publisher calibration objects specify millimetres. The issue and future reference required eligible activity intervals, unflagged optical coordinates and in-view projections. At 120 ms, optical samples 14 and 15 after the issue row were interpolated at weights 0.6 and 0.4. Endpoint error is Euclidean distance to that reference. Displacement error compares the predicted change from the tracked issue point with the optical change from its own issue point. Equal activity and joint cells were averaged within person, then equally across people.

The original per-joint image schedules supplied 293,462 common camera keys. The 3D history criteria admitted 288,620 forecasts, and 202,251 had scoreable references across 86 people. Current reconstructed points averaged 52.06 mm from the corresponding optical references. Earlier two-dimensional work had already used this cohort. The source-selected predictions for the three-dimensional comparison were frozen before scoring the new outcome.

Simultaneous complete poses

The independent per-point timers produced zero common original issue frames containing all 12 sites. A supplementary test used every synchronized frame with all sites available at issue and sufficient history. The weights stayed fixed. Among 396,536 frame pairs, 5,404 met both full-pose history rules and 5,138 frames from 84 people had complete independent references. This covers 1.3% of source frame pairs. Each point and frame within a named activity, then each activity and participant, received equal weight.

Muscle and single-camera comparisons

On the same ten overground test people, the clean optical comparison covered 25,064 point windows. Adding real EMG increased mean error by 0.74 mm, paired 95% interval +0.58 to +0.93 mm, with higher error in all ten people. In 23,278 separate windows with synthetic tracking degradation, muscle input reduced error by 2.40 mm. The source exported filtered marker and EMG signals. Its whole-trial preprocessing limits online interpretation.

Two people and four previously recorded camera views supplied 21,593 independent single-camera comparisons. Optical vectors relative to the hip midpoint were rotated into the published camera axes, with no fitted scale or alignment. Spatial endpoint and displacement errors were 130.65 and 57.23 mm, against persistence at 119.65 and 38.22 mm. Errors in change of distance from the hips, which is independent of axis orientation, were 24.44 and 13.18 mm respectively. These two recorded people establish neither population-wide accuracy nor a physical home-webcam result.
