Force plate and waist watch forecasts

A force plate measures how hard the floor pushes up. A watch at the waist measures how it speeds up and turns. The exercise lets you change what either device has just measured and see four forecasts for the next tenth of a second. Each device uses its own timeline.

Try the exercise

  1. Open the exercise. Choose Force plate or Waist watch. The chart shows an example with invented readings. Each of its ten values averages 20 milliseconds. Together they cover the last fifth of a second.
  2. Open Edit earlier readings and change a number. The forecast table updates. With Waist watch selected, use Input channel to choose which of the six readings to edit. The scores from people’s recordings stay in Results on recorded data.
  3. Choose Restore example to reset the device you are viewing. Open Results on recorded data to compare the methods on the same recordings. For floor force, you can show results separately for windows where the last 20-millisecond average was at least 20 newtons or below 20 newtons.

Read the two forecasts

The floor prediction adds up the push over the next tenth of a second. This is called impulse. A steady push of 300 newtons for a tenth of a second gives 30 newton-seconds. The waist prediction gives the average turning rate of the watch around each of its three axes. It uses radians per second, a unit of turning speed.

On recordings from three people held aside for testing, keeping the latest force average steady gave the smallest overall floor error, about 2.98 newton-seconds. At the waist, the best learned method lowered the error from about 0.52 to 0.31 radians per second.

The exercise and both guides are included in the Stage Guide download. Open the Stage Guide launcher, choose the movement model stage, and select Forecast force and waist rotation. The browser runs the calculations on the device you use to open it.
