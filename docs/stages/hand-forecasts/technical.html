Methods and scoring

Three fixed NeuroTranslate models use 200 ms of prior events from 96 ordered primary motor cortex electrodes to forecast 100 ms ahead. Both blends scale the same outputs. The reach-weighted fit assigns half of its calibration objective to displacement of at least 20 mm. See the original report for the six inputs, coefficient fit and calibration recordings.

Ending position error is the distance between predicted and measured endpoints. Distance error is the absolute difference between the separate straight-line distance prediction and the measured displacement magnitude. In each group, averages give trials equal weight and include all three fitted seeds. Measured displacement bands are assigned after forecasts are issued. The bars share a zero origin within the current selection and scale to its largest error.

Results and scope

On 45,324 frozen forecasts from 2,911 trials, overall ending position error was 3.225 mm with the published blend, 3.355 mm with the reach-weighted blend and 6.111 mm with staying put. Staying put had the lowest error under 2 mm. The original models had the lowest error at 20 mm or more.

Reach weighting lowered endpoint error on movements of at least 20 mm by 0.699 mm and raised it under 2 mm by 0.233 mm relative to the published blend. Overall endpoint error rose 0.130 mm (paired 95% interval 0.120 to 0.140 mm). Distance error fell 0.030 mm (paired 95% interval for improvement 0.017 to 0.044 mm). The study resampled whole trials 4,000 times for the intervals. The held recording is a different day from the same macaque. New animals, electrode layouts and live control require separate tests.
