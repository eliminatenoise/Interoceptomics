Select a stage and a companion

Open Human movement for the recorded wrist trial or Animal learning · Navigation for the rat recording. The guide has 50 steps in 14 groups. In Stage or task, choose a group, then choose a named step. Use Previous step and Next step, or the left and right arrow keys outside an input control, to move between steps.

Under Explore this step, select a Model to explore. Choose Open here to embed the viewer. Close model returns focus to the guide control. Open in a separate tab opens the viewer on its own.

Copy link to this view stores the step, selected companion, Task time and View angle. It also stores Limb and Reference where available. The wrist trial requires verified recording files and stores the trace sample, forecast cutoff and input category. If verification fails, choose Try again in the wrist recording before copying. The companion remains closed and its controls stay in its own viewer. If the clipboard is unavailable, copy the displayed address.

Movement mirror controls and measurements

Open the hosted Movement mirror preview with internet access. Full and Lite share the same controls. Lite downloads smaller anatomy files. Press C for the motion recorder. Select Start camera to request video. Use Follow to select the limb the recorder tracks. Under Source, choose Recorded video, open a Local video file and select Play video. For Timed pictures, choose your images, enter distinct times in seconds in the displayed file order, starting at zero or later, and select Track pictures.

Choose Movement forecast or enter “forecast a tracked point” in the F Command box. This closes the recorder. In the forecast view, select Source again. Use Start camera for live tracking. Open a Local video file and use Play selected clip for recorded video. For Timestamped stills, select the images, enter distinct times in seconds in the displayed file order, starting at zero or later, and choose Track keyframes. Body follows selects the joints the tracked movement drives on the virtual body. Point to forecast selects an image point independently. The forecast uses five ordered images with gaps of at most 0.24 s and the point visible in the last and at least two preceding images. It projects the point 120 ms ahead. The stay-put prediction uses the last tracked position. Both predictions are scored in image pixels against the same later tracked position, within 40 ms of the target time. Once the selected limb is tracked, Save take keeps up to eight seconds of its movement in the tab. Select Open Command box and enter “replay my take” to replay it.

Press F to open the Command box. Enter a supported virtual action such as “lift my right wrist above my shoulder” or enter “replay my take” to repeat a saved movement. The speech option uses on-device recognition where the browser supports it. The wrist trial below uses a separate measurement. The preview's accuracy test uses recorded images. Two wrist angles after a visual cue

At From brain signals to movement · Brain2Body, move Time from the visual cue from 0.5 seconds before to 2.5 seconds after the cue. The scalp trace is voltage at C3. The forearm trace is the difference between two electrode readings. Both use microvolts (µV). Two encoders on the wrist device measured bend and side tilt in degrees.

Select Last input, after the cue and Measurements the model used. Compare the saved forecasts with the two angles measured 250 milliseconds later. Show this outcome on the traces moves the recording cursor to that time. The displayed trial was excluded from the 959-trial comparison across three people. In that comparison, adding forearm signals to wrist angles increased the root mean square error from 5.432° to 5.866° across both wrist axes, with each person weighted equally. Hand forecasts from ordered cortical electrodes

Open Hand forecasts from brain recordings and choose Open here. Compare 100 ms forecasts from one held macaque recording day against a zero-displacement baseline. Select a measured displacement band and endpoint or distance error. The same comparison is available in the downloaded Stage Guide.

Handle force, Area 2 and hand displacement

Open Compare hand forecasts with measured force and choose Open here. Six forecasts use the same 624 rows in 74 held trials from one archived Area 2 recording. Adding measured force to motion reduced trial-weighted 100 ms endpoint error from 2.396 to 2.276 mm. Adding Area 2 activity to both raised it to 2.328 mm overall. The interface shows paired ranges, both error measures and retrospective displacement strata. The downloaded Stage Guide includes this comparison.

Acceleration, ground force and impulse

In Human movement, choose Open the walking recording. Select a Wearable sensor and a Forecast to compare. The three axes give acceleration in sensor coordinates. Compare the forecast with the reading 120 ms after the last input. The parking-lot walk was a training example. In the separate 3D schematic, Limb, Task time and View angle control the illustration. Walking excerpt and source terms.

Walking forecasts with measured force reports a separate 0.6 m/s study of 16 walkers with an above-knee prosthesis. Cameras tracked heel markers and plates measured foot load. Given 200 ms of history, adding force reduced the error in the next 100 ms of vertical impulse for every walker. The mean error fell from 16.35 to 7.13 newton-seconds (N·s). Impulse is force integrated over time. The linked offline model starts from invented inputs. In Standing and sitting force forecast, readers can edit an invented bilateral force history and compare three forecasts. The recorded force check admitted two of three reserved people, providing 230 windows. Both studies measure external load. Neither supplies an account of how the movement felt.

Open Compare walking forecasts by pass count and choose Open here. Compare signed force-impulse MAE and new-load Brier error at five inference depths on the same validation walks.

Open Forecast force and waist rotation to run the browser checkpoint on generated input. Compare 100 ms signed force impulse and waist rotation-rate predictions, each on its device’s own timestamps. The online and downloaded guide include the same exercise and recorded aggregate scores.

W-track positions, counts and test trips

At 0.2 · Navigation, choose a Training trip. Under Prediction to mark on the maps, compare Past positions with Past positions + spikes. The models use five camera positions over the preceding second. The second adds counts of unit firing in four 250 ms bins. The requested forecast horizon is 250 ms. Choose Reveal the later camera reading to see the first stored two-dimensional position at or after that time, up to 50 ms later. The table gives the miss in centimetres against that reading.

The browser applies two fitted ridge regressions, which limit the size of the fitted weights. The three worked trips come from the 24 training trips. Across nine later test trips, weighted equally, the mean position errors were 2.01 cm with positions and 2.24 cm after adding spikes. Camera frame replays positions and two units from another running period. The straight-corridor diagram below them follows a programmed path on its own clock. Trace a pathway and inspect its measurements

Choose Animal learning, then Back to human function. Myoception is the programme’s term for sensing muscle-driven movement and the feedback that accompanies it, including the sense of body position, task-relevant touch and other sensations linked to the action. Trace a signal from a muscle locates sensory relays in the Backslash atlas. Count the spikes from a muscle spindle opens a separate recording from one sensory axon during passive stretch of a rat calf. The nerve was sampled at 20,000 Hz and tendon force and apparatus position at 2,000 Hz. A human muscle/knee rhythm comparison uses separate device windows, which support repetition-rate comparisons and leave individual bursts and bends unaligned.

Visceroception addresses sensing conditions inside organs. Compare the anatomical route with Read the heartbeat and breathing traces. A common cursor in a resting recording samples the electrocardiogram (ECG), chest accelerometer voltage and breathing-band voltage. A report from the person would be needed to establish their experience. Both sensory steps offer Follow the exercise and pressure loop, an anatomical route assembled from different preparations. Backslash atlas technical edition.

In Neural Composite · Follow one stride from muscles to the floor, 52 skin markers, eight right-leg muscle electrical channels and two force plates describe the same walks. Group means align by percent of the slow stride. The contributing walkers may differ between curves. The proposed composite would link brain and spinal signals to muscle and movement in one action. Neither neural measurement exists for this stride. Compare firing and movement on a recorded trip uses a separate rat study and clock.

Compare zebrafish tail forecasts. Compare four forecasts of tail-angle spread against later camera measurements in one zebrafish recording. The program logged the visual command on its own clock. Select this companion in MotorSensation.

Compare tail forecasts across 12 recordings. Compare seven methods across 12 later recordings, giving each recording equal weight. Inspect the effect of adding the current command to a camera-only model or using an older command history. Select the 12-recording companion in MotorSensation.

Read the proposed experiments

The human groups cover movement mapping, hand dexterity, multi-limb coordination and two timing or coordination tasks. The animal roadmap introduces five aims and 22 numbered steps across Stages 0–5. Stage 0 treats movement, navigation and replay. Stage 1 proposes an independent functional test of a specified human-related feature in a model organism. Stage 2 distinguishes the presence of donor tissue from evidence that it contributes to a function in the host. Stage 3 considers transfer between animals. Stage 4 considers guidance from a software agent. Stage 5 asks how a defined impairment or a change in the environment would affect performance. Each proposal links to its technical script page under Evidence and limits.

Where Reference appears, compare Matched, Delayed and Off while adjusting Task time. The software draws the reference and moving point. Establishing learning would require independent measurements after guidance stops, matched practice and a later retention test. The stage operates no live device and supplies no result for the proposed biological interventions or transfer of skill.

Launch the download

From the experiment guide choose Download the guide for offline use (ZIP). Extract the whole ZIP. Launch Windows.cmd, macOS.command or Linux.sh in the extracted folder with Python 3.10 or later and a current browser. Keep the terminal open. If a browser tab does not appear, paste the printed local address into your browser. Press Ctrl+C in the terminal to stop the server.

The local package contains the stage recordings, bundled readers, Backslash atlas and both script PDFs. A copied view points to the public guide. External NeuroTranslate and NeuroSurge links, original publications and separate downloads need internet access. Technical script · Simple script.
