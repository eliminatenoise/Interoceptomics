# One right wrist and one visual cue

Jorge German Perez Blanco and Javier Mauricio Anteliz Ortiz published the **EEG-EMG-WristMotion Dataset, version 3**, under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. A visual target cued a participant to move a cursor with the right wrist. The recording contains scalp and right-forearm voltages alongside two wrist encoder angles. The participant’s electrode coordinates, spinal and sensory nerve activity, muscle force, joint torque and spoken words are absent from this example. [Version 3 dataset](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29666735.v3) · [Data descriptor](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07287-z) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Follow the same rows

[The three-second excerpt](wrist-pointing.json) comes from participant 41, run 03, block 1, trial 1. The NeuroSurge extractor selected stored rows 2331 to 3867 inclusive, preserved the raw voltages and angles, subtracted the paired forearm electrode voltages, and retained the event and sensor provenance. The visual cue is the source marker `TrialInitMovStam`, target code 1, at row 2587. The two wrist encoders update at a nominal 100 Hz on a 512 Hz stored clock. Identical adjacent angles are valid. The instrument and filtering delays are unknown. This guide uses the exact public excerpt, SHA-256 `578184a108d7682c37a4f409ce4cd7535b33d203007f39ca0f5d28831ffde41f`. [Public source excerpt](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroSurge/blob/981434f03f54b9259d9c69eac6fd8215fdeaf143/inputs/activity/wrist-pointing.json) · [Extraction code](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroSurge/blob/981434f03f54b9259d9c69eac6fd8215fdeaf143/tools/extract-wrist-activity.py)

The two displayed voltage traces are C3 on the scalp and M3L minus M3R on the right forearm. The raw values are in microvolts. Positive wrist extension is bend towards the back of the hand. Positive radial deviation is tilt towards the thumb. Both angular traces use the same vertical scale. The cursor follows stored samples. Drawing lines between samples does not add measurements.

## How the forecasts were calculated

[The saved forecasts](wrist-forecasts.json) come from three copies of the NeuroTranslation Transformer, each trained on six people. They use the most recent 256 recorded rows and stop 128 rows before the two angles they aim to predict. Six successive cutoff times cover 0.50 to 1.75 seconds after the cue. The models use averages of the wrist angles, the angles at the cutoff and the time since the cue. The second model also uses the root mean square of four right-forearm electrode differences in each group of 16 rows. The third adds eight channel averages, one per scalp electrode in each group. No model sees a future sensor value. The selected public trial was excluded from training, assessment and the comparison's signal-swapping controls. Its table shows what these trained models did on one known demonstration. [Frozen models, source fields, formulas and predictor](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/tree/117ffa001fb10086854ae4ec4d365c7c2121786f/benchmarks/paired-wrist-forecast)

Each forecast row includes the first and last source rows used, the later row used for checking, the measured encoder values, all three predictions and the input hashes. The displayed change subtracts the measured angle at the cutoff from the measured angle 250 ms later. The displayed miss is the absolute difference between predicted and measured angles on that later row. The original study reports an error over people and both axes, weighted equally by person, with each miss squared before averaging and the square root taken last. One trial’s misses and the study score answer different questions. [Study results](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/blob/117ffa001fb10086854ae4ec4d365c7c2121786f/benchmarks/paired-wrist-forecast/README.md)
