    Evidence and limits

    What remains unshown

    The 3D model holds 121 of the atlas’s 141 regions. Twenty have no surface. Being in the model does not make a region visible in every view, and says nothing about what it does. The atlas gives no voxels of their own to the small nuclei that regulate the organs or to the separate deep nuclei of the cerebellum, and it does not mark the working fields of the cortex, so none of these is drawn.

    Some regions are only parts of larger surfaces and cannot be lifted out alone. The habenula, the pineal body and the zona incerta are left grey.

    Two stray voxels

    The atlas labels two voxels as hypothalamus far from the rest of it. They are in the model and are not drawn unless you ask. Whether the atlas is right about them is not settled here.

    Each 0.5 mm cube contributes 0.125 mm³. Its centre falls left of, on, or right of R = 0. The count spans the entire annotated grid and includes only the exact selected ID. Child labels have their own IDs.

    This atlas labels mainly the left side. A zero on the right means this grid assigns no voxels there to that ID. Boundaries follow the grid and give an approximate anatomical volume.
