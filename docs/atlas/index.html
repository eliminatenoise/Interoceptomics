    Evidence and limits

    What the model leaves out

    The 3D model holds all 141 of the atlas’s regions. Twenty of them are drawn only when you ask: choose one and Show it alone, or choose Show white matter and fluid spaces to see fifteen of them in place. Being in the model does not make a region visible in every view, and says nothing about what it does. The atlas gives no voxels of their own to the small nuclei that regulate the organs or to the separate deep nuclei of the cerebellum, and it does not mark the working fields of the cortex, so none of these is drawn.

    Some regions are only parts of larger surfaces and cannot be lifted out alone. The habenula, the pineal body and the zona incerta are left grey.

    Two stray voxels

    The atlas labels two voxels as hypothalamus far from the rest of it. They are in the model and are not drawn unless you ask. Whether the atlas is right about them is not settled here.

    Three flat cuts through the atlas

    The atlas is a grid of half-millimetre cubes, called voxels, and each voxel carries the number of one region. These three cuts cross at one voxel. Pick a tile, or use the arrow keys; hold Shift to move ten voxels at a time.

    Each colour stands for one atlas number, and colours can repeat. The atlas’s own section images and the MRI template it was drawn on are not shown here.

    Find a region

    Each side offered has voxels with that atlas number. The crosshair lands on the one nearest their average position. A region’s named parts have atlas numbers of their own.

    Each voxel adds 0.125 mm³, and counts as left, midline or right by where its centre lies. The count covers the whole atlas grid and only this region’s own atlas number: its named parts have numbers of their own and are not included.

    The atlas labels mainly the left side: a zero on the right means it puts none of this region’s voxels there. Borders follow the grid, so the volume is approximate.

    Coronal cuts run parallel to the face, sagittal cuts parallel to the side of the head, axial cuts horizontal. Letters at the edges of each cut: L left, R right, P back, A front, I down, S up. The R, A and S numbers give a voxel’s centre in millimetres.
