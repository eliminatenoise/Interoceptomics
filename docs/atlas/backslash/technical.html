Backslash of the Brain · Technical guide

Backslash of the Brain is an Interoceptomics atlas of anatomical landmarks for muscle feedback, organ sensation and motor control. Its circuit guide connects landmarks across the brain, spinal cord and organs using published evidence.

Open the offline atlas

  1. Download Backslash-Atlas.zip. Extract the entire archive and open the resulting Backslash-Atlas folder. You need Python 3.10 or newer and a current browser.
  2. Open Windows.cmd on Windows or macOS.command on macOS. On Linux, run sh Linux.sh in the extracted folder. From that folder you can also run python3 launch.py or python launch.py, using the command for your Python installation.
  3. Leave the launcher window open. If the browser stays closed, paste the full address printed by the launcher into the browser. The address uses a new local port when needed. Press Ctrl+C in the launcher window to stop the atlas.

The launcher serves the model and PDF atlas on your computer. Use it to open the viewer. External source pages require internet. The launch was verified in Linux Chromium. Windows and macOS launcher files were checked at source level.

Explore the three anatomy views

View Anatomy and controls
Brain The default Medial left hemisphere shows the inner surface of the left half of the Allen reference brain. The front is on the right at the starting angle. Use Brain landmark to select Sensory cortex landmark, Thalamic body relay · VPL or Insula under the opercula. The ventral posterolateral nucleus (VPL) is a group of thalamic cells that passes body signals to sensory cortex. The opercula are folds that cover the insula. Drag and scroll to rotate and zoom when 3D is available. 12° diagonal view tilts the display. Reset view restores the starting view. A reference image remains available without 3D graphics.
Brainstem sections Five cross-sections of the right half of one human donor’s brainstem, including the midline. Choose a Section level, such as Upper pons · parabrachial region. The pons is part of the brainstem. The parabrachial region contains a group of nerve cells in its upper part. Use Previous section, Next section or Full-size plate ↗.
Cord & organs Spinal cord, heart, colon and urinary bladder in the female Human Reference Atlas assembly. Choose Organ selection. Drag or use Rotate left and Rotate right, then Reset view. Open the labelled organ views without 3D graphics opens front and back reference images for the selected structure.

Where offered in Brain, switch between 3D surface and Coronal section, a cut facing the front of the head. Each image pixel spans 0.5 mm in the displayed axes. More posterior and More anterior move through the three available slices. Orange marks cells of the atlas grid assigned to the selected label. Inspect a highlighted voxel from this slice in all three planes opens the full atlas at a highlighted cell in that slice. The surface link Inspect this atlas voxel in all three planes opens the landmark’s reference cell.

The separate Tissue plates view uses Tissue reference and Full-size plate ↗ to show supporting images. The captions distinguish microscopy, other anatomical preparations and teaching diagrams, and identify the species where the plate shows tissue.

Follow a circuit

Select Muscle sensation to cortex under Follow a circuit. Start at Muscle spindle and tendon organ. Next opens Dorsal root and spinal cord. Follow the subsequent stops through the medulla, VPL and sensory cortex. Use Previous, Next or a named stop to move. Selecting a view button or a landmark returns you to free exploration.

Myoception means sensing muscle-driven movement and its feedback, including signals about body position and touch. Visceroception means sensing conditions inside internal organs, such as stretch as the bladder fills. Organ sensation to forebrain follows incoming organ feedback. Cortex to skeletal muscle and Central commands to organs follow outgoing control. Stretch reflex and coordination shows the local spinal response and the separate branch toward the cerebellum.

Integration describes how feedback and outgoing control work together. Exercise and arterial pressure traces how working muscle signals help adjust cardiovascular control and how arterial stretch provides feedback. Other examples are Bladder filling and emptying, A local gut reflex and Body state and selecting a response.

Sources and limits

The left half of the reference brain and its labelled grid come from the Allen Human Reference Atlas. Coulombe’s brainstem plates are images of tissue from a different donor. The cord and organ surfaces retain the positions of the female Human Reference Atlas assembly. These sources use separate reference frames. Colours identify atlas regions and published associations. Circuit steps illustrate relationships among structures. Neural firing and individual functional borders are outside the measurements made by this atlas.
