Where the anatomy comes from

Each structure opens the view that supports it. A surface marks the shape of a labelled atlas region. A section can show a boundary too small for that surface. A pathway description connects findings from separate experiments.

Left brain in three dimensions

The Allen Human Reference Atlas – 3D, 2020, version 1.0.0 supplies a 0.5 mm annotation grid. The interactive brain retains the source-left coordinates and parcel identities. Coloured areas can share roles. A regional label such as “precentral gyrus” locates a landmark for motor cortex; it does not establish the functional boundary of M1.

Right brainstem under the microscope

Coulombe and colleagues prepared transverse sections parallel to the pontomesencephalic junction from the right hemi-brainstem of a 61-year-old woman. The sections include the midline. The plus and minus distances are measured within the authors’ frame. Cell-body and fibre stains reveal different features. These images retain their original scales and printed labels. Source study and methods · CC BY 4.0

Spinal cord and organ surfaces

Kristen Browne and Heidi Schlehlein’s female united reference-organ set, Human Reference Atlas v1.10, supplies these shapes. The four selected node families retain the original mesh coordinates and parent transforms within that assembly. They comprise 59 meshes. This viewer changes the display colours. These organs do not share a coordinate system with the Allen brain or the donor brainstem sections.
