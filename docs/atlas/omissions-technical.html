The twenty regions drawn only when you ask · Technical edition

The Interoceptomics Atlas Viewer draws a 3D model of the left half of the human brain from a published brain atlas, a map that divides the brain into named regions. Twenty of the atlas’s regions are drawn only when you ask, and together they hold almost a third of the atlas’s labelled voxels (the voxels that belong to a region). This page lists them, states how to show them and how their surfaces were made.

Which regions they are

  • The white matter of the forebrain and five of its named bundles of nerve fibres. White matter is the bundles of nerve fibres that join the regions to one another, and the forebrain is the largest part of the brain: the two cerebral hemispheres (the large folded masses that make up most of each half of the brain) and the parts deep between them.
  • Nine fluid spaces: the ventricles and the canals that join them, which are cavities that hold fluid, not tissue.
  • The four regions of the smell pathway, which carries smell from the nose into the brain.
  • The claustrum, a thin sheet of grey matter (tissue where the bodies of the nerve cells lie) between the insula (a stretch of the brain’s outer layer, folded deep inside the side of the brain) and the putamen (a region of grey matter deep inside each half of the brain).

None of them carries a colour: the viewer’s three colours mark the regions that this project links to movement and touch (blue), to the automatic running of the organs (copper), and to where the two meet (purple), and these twenty are drawn in grey.

The 3D model has 64 surfaces. A surface is the skin that the viewer draws around a region, or around several regions merged together. Every one of the atlas’s 141 regions is drawn, on a surface of its own or as part of a larger one, except five small nuclei (clusters of nerve cells) that lie inside the thalamus (a mass of grey matter deep in the middle of the brain).

Why they stay off until you ask

They are not small. A voxel is one 0.5 mm cube of the atlas grid, and a labelled voxel is one that belongs to a region.

They stay off until you ask, so that the first view is as it was before these surfaces were added and nothing more is downloaded. When they are on, the white matter of the forebrain and the fluid spaces are see-through. Drawn solid they would hide what the viewer is for: the white matter wraps the coloured regions, and the fluid spaces on the midline lie in front of the thalamus and the brainstem when the brain is seen from its inner face. The midline is the boundary between the left and right halves of the head, and the brainstem is the stalk at the base of the brain that joins the rest of the brain to the spinal cord.

How their surfaces were made

The surfaces come from the same atlas grid as the rest of the model. Of the 54 regions that can be shown alone, thirty have their surfaces in the main model file (the file of the 3D model that the viewer loads first), these twenty have a file each, which loads when asked for, and four have a file each: VPL, VPM, the red nucleus and the parietal operculum. The twenty were made the same way as the thirty: a surface drawn around the region’s voxels on the 0.5 mm grid and closed at the midline, where points that lay beyond the midline were moved onto it by at most 0.25 mm, then smoothed slightly; the smoothing moved no point more than 0.125 mm.

  • Where one of these regions touches a region that already had a surface, the atlas gives the two the same boundary. After smoothing, their surfaces stay within a quarter of a millimetre of each other there.
  • The 3D model is the left half of the grid, up to the midline. Five of these regions also have voxels right of the midline, and those voxels are not drawn: the white matter of the forebrain (7,832 voxels), the third ventricle (1,341), the fourth ventricle (953), the cerebral aqueduct (224) and the anterior commissure (15).
  • At the bottom of the grid the atlas starts the central canal 1 mm lower than the medulla (the lowest part of the brainstem), and for its lowest 2.5 mm no other region lies within 1.5 mm of it. The surface of the central canal therefore sticks out below the brainstem there.
  • The viewer’s list of regions keeps a proposed colour group, Integration (the purple group, which the viewer calls “where the two meet”), for three of the twenty: the olfactory bulb, the anterior olfactory nucleus and the piriform cortex. It is a proposal, and nothing is drawn for it: the line under each name reads No colour group, and only Evidence and limits mentions the proposal.

Whose names and numbers these are

The four groups in the table are this project’s. The names, abbreviations and numbers are the Allen Institute’s, from its table of regions. A copy of that table travels with the viewer: sources/allen-version1/voxel_count.csv. The voxel counts were recounted from the atlas grid for this page, and they equal the counts in that table.
