Twenty regions are drawn only when you ask, and together they are a third of the atlas

The 3D model has 64 surfaces. Every one of the atlas’s 141 regions is drawn, on a surface of its own or as part of a larger one, except five small nuclei that lie inside the thalamus. Twenty of the regions stay off the screen until you ask for them: the white matter of the forebrain and five of its named bundles of nerve fibres, nine fluid spaces (the ventricles and canals, cavities that hold fluid, not tissue), the four regions of the smell pathway, and the claustrum, a thin sheet of grey matter between the insula and the putamen. None of them carries a colour.

They are not small. Together they hold 2,255,724 of the atlas’s 6,999,201 labelled voxels (a voxel is one half-millimetre cube of the grid), and the white matter of the forebrain alone holds 2,089,673. They stay off until you ask, so that the first view is as it was and nothing more is downloaded: the fifteen the button draws are a 36 MB download, the white matter of the forebrain alone 32.2 MB. When they are on, the white matter of the forebrain and the fluid spaces are see-through, because drawn solid they would hide what the viewer is for: the white matter wraps the coloured regions, and the fluid spaces on the midline lie in front of the thalamus and the brainstem when the brain is seen from its inner face.

Three ways to see them

  • Show white matter and fluid spaces, under Reset view, draws fifteen of them in place, in every view: the white matter, its fibre bundles and the fluid spaces, a 36 MB download in all. They show best with Deep regions.
  • To see any one of the twenty on its own, type its name, abbreviation or atlas number into Find a region, pick it from the list and choose Show it alone. Its file loads when you pick it.
  • To read them voxel by voxel, choose Open three flat cuts, then Find a region.

How their surfaces were made

From the same atlas grid as the rest of the model, and the same way as the thirty regions that have a surface of their own in the main model file: a surface drawn around the region’s voxels on the half-millimetre grid and closed at the midline, then smoothed slightly, so that no point moved more than 0.125 mm. Where one of these regions touches a region that already had a surface, the atlas gives the two the same boundary, and after smoothing their surfaces stay within a quarter of a millimetre of each other there.

The 3D model is the left half of the grid, up to the midline. Five of these regions also have voxels right of the midline, and those are not drawn: the white matter of the forebrain (7,832 voxels), the third ventricle (1,341), the fourth ventricle (953), the cerebral aqueduct (224) and the anterior commissure (15).

At the bottom of the grid the atlas starts the central canal 1 mm lower than the medulla, and for its lowest 2.5 mm no other region lies within 1.5 mm of it. So its surface sticks out below the brainstem there.

The viewer’s list of regions keeps a proposed colour group for three of the twenty (the olfactory bulb, the anterior olfactory nucleus and the piriform cortex). It is a proposal, and nothing is drawn for it.

The four groups in the table are this project’s. The names, abbreviations and numbers are the Allen Institute’s, from its table of regions. A copy of that table travels with the viewer: sources/allen-version1/voxel_count.csv. The voxel counts were recounted from the atlas grid for this page, and they equal the counts in that table.
