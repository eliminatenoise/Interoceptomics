What the numbers measure

ECG lead II records electrical activity through skin electrodes. A band follows breathing and an accelerometer on the chest picks up vibration. All numbers here are sensor output in millivolts. Each displayed chest reading combines the preceding 40 milliseconds of raw samples; the target combines only samples after the cutoff, from +40 to +80 milliseconds.

The models learned from five people. These are the five other recordings used to test them. The calibration for each person uses their own earlier readings between 60 and 80 seconds. “Earlier ECG” uses the electrical signal from 5.012 seconds earlier, with its own fitted coefficients. The error table gives the root mean squared error for the visible interval. Smaller is better.

Across the full test intervals, adding ECG helped two people and worsened the average error by 8.76% relative to each person’s chest-and-breathing model. One short excerpt or a single forecast can give a different impression. These sensors do not record how a heartbeat felt.
